## 2026-09-02 — Panel: the walk — the book is what the record's descent reaches, the empty book is a signed claim, and currency is `serves`

**Status: the fix panel the slice-36 round owed, and the design the fix
builds.** Four Opus angles (the replaced-mechanism inventory, adversarial,
simplicity/consolidation, practicality), all probe-backed, three with
prototype builds run against the suite and the round's seven exploits
(`scratch/panel36/`: the brief, the runner, and one report per angle). The
question as filed had two roots — what venue-derived object bounds the
evidence path's offered book, and what the pin guard's real condition is —
and the angles converged on one design from four directions, then split on
one price, which this entry settles.

**What the angles found, each probe-proven:**

- **A1 was never refused by a check.** `committedLogFor` has no venue check
  on main either; what refused the retired key's forgery there was the
  walk-back's sequence ceiling sitting at the anchor, incidentally. The
  branch moved the ceiling to `latest` and the promise silently lapsed.
  Three of the branch's Root-A changes were held up by ONE test, named for
  the seat rather than the evidence path, which the branch had rewritten to
  keep green — that rewrite was the A4 regression, absorbed. (Inventory: a
  twelve-mutation sweep; two promises had no test at all.)
- **The narrowing (A-D2) is incoherent as a floor**: for a fresh heir the
  anchor IS the record's last commitment, so a floor there empties the
  walk-back (nine tests fail); its prototyped half closes the wipe at a
  pinned seat only and nothing at a genesis seat. **A Venue identity query
  (A-D3) buys nothing**: `lastCommitmentInForce(chain, venue, at - 1n)`
  stepped backward enumerates the backing's in-force commitments with the
  four interface methods that exist, and identity against its answer settles
  MORE than "was this witnessed" — and placed inside `committedLogFor` for
  every reader, the query deletes the unwitnessed-rewrite fault proof
  `isRewrittenHistory` was written to keep. **Spec-literal (A-D4) bricks the
  single-operator configuration** with no adversary: an own drop at a seat
  whose E names no replacement rule leaves the remedy set empty.
- **The exhibit walk cannot be steered.** Eleven attacks (one exhibit where
  three are needed, duplicates, out of order, a gap, an older carrying state,
  a forged state at a real sequence, a carrying commitment exhibited as
  dropped, a lying exhibit, nothing offered, too many exhibits) — ten refused,
  the honest one accepted. `stateRoot` is injective, so a commitment that
  carries the backing cannot be exhibited as dropped, and every step is the
  venue's answer, never the caller's. Across term boundaries it is equally
  tight: a retired key's later commitments for other backings are not steps,
  a re-appointed key's two terms both place, a commitment at an effective
  index is in neither.
- **Root B's claim — held link ∧ pin ≠ latest ⟹ the tail is dead — holds in
  every configuration that can arise.** A lead-time seat cannot hold a tail
  (no door opens before force); a live writer's own `commit` rewrites its own
  pin; the losing twin's tail is superseded. The one exception is a SECOND
  process for the same key dropping the backing (the sharded key, W3): the
  tail is unsuperseded and the repair kills it with its receipts. That is a
  one-writer violation, priced; the warning it lacks belongs at `commit`,
  and it cannot be given there because a process never scans a backing it
  did not register — a boot-rule sentence, not a mechanism. B-i and B-ii are
  behaviourally indistinguishable; one-mechanism picks `serves` itself.
- **The two "no-op" tests and the losing twin repair in one call** under a
  currency-keyed guard; under the branch, one un-serves a live operator and
  the other has no public repair at all.
- **A seventh blocker** (practicality): an heir whose predecessor's last
  dropped the backing, that walked back, committed a carrying state and then
  restarts, is refused by every one of seven calls — the anchor arm needs the
  anchor to carry, the evidence arms need the record's last to drop, and
  once the heir has committed neither holds. Same regression as A4, one
  term later.
- **An exhibit is every backing's whole log at that commitment** — the root
  hashes snapshot digests and nothing else, so "carries nothing for EUR" is
  provable only by handing over the other backings' logs. At 100 backings of
  1,000 entries an exhibit is 19.2 MB, and one exhibit per intervening
  commitment is the walk's price: fine in every own-term flow (one drop), a
  lever in the hostile-predecessor case (one publication buys one exhibit,
  bounded by the lead time, every drop a provable fault), and IMPOSSIBLE for
  the empty book at a seat with history — the door cannot tell "I never had
  this backing" from "I lost its book" (both read identically: anchor
  undefined, record's last drops it, ledger empty), so any door that accepts
  the honest new backing accepts the wipe, and any door that refuses the wipe
  refuses the new backing. Information-theoretic, not a bug.
- **The 35d guard "an heir may not license its own commitment" is not held
  by a mechanism.** At a genesis seat the two-call resume loads the empty
  book first, so the prefix loop compares against nothing and a witnessed
  own truncation is adopted TODAY; at a successor seat the fuller book
  survives only because B1's pin regression then un-serves the seat. Its
  real content — an UNWITNESSED own-signed state cannot be licensed — is
  exactly what identity against the walk keeps.

**The design, one sentence per piece:**

1. **`takeOver` is one walk.** The book a seat takes on is the one the
   record's last in-force commitment stands on: the content of the last
   in-force commitment that CARRIES this backing, reached by exhibiting each
   later one as a served state that drops it — matched to the record by
   identity at every step (`recorded`, the generalized `requireDroppedBy`,
   now asked of the offered state too, which is the venue check
   `committedLogFor` never had) — and the empty book only where the walk
   runs out of record. The ordinary handover, the lead-time takeover and the
   resume are the zero-exhibit case; §C2b's walk-back is the k-exhibit case.
   The anchor arm, the raise's held-seat gate, `standsOn`, the pin no-op
   guard, `termOf` at this door and the `seat.from === 0n` special case are
   deleted: every `takeOver` either serves or throws, which is what removes
   the strand and the seventh blocker by construction. Signature:
   `takeOver(backing, served?, ...dropped)`, newest first — source-compatible
   with every existing call.
2. **Currency is `serves`.** `const current = this.serves(held)` read once
   before anything mutates decides `keep`, the `restore` and nothing else;
   the pin a `takeOver` writes is the record's identity, always. `reSync`'s
   four jobs become one predicate, and it is the one every door already asks.
3. **The empty book at a seat with history is a signed claim, never a
   door's acceptance.** `commit({ opening })` roots a registered, in-force,
   unserved backing whose held book is EMPTY under an explicit per-call
   acknowledgement — the exact dual of `dropping`, strict in both directions
   the same way — and seats it on that commitment. A true claim (a new
   backing) is growth from nothing, which `isRewrittenHistory` already reads
   as no fault; a false one (a backing the record carried) is a witnessed
   rewritten history, signed, provable by any holder of the earlier state
   forever. The claim moves from the one place it is unprovable (a door
   reading a root) to the one place it is (the operator's own signature on
   the record). The walk still licenses the empty book where the record
   genuinely runs out — the F2 heir, and a depth-one history — so the two
   `c2-ergo-venue` fixtures and the never-carried test keep their shape.
4. **Every refusal names the honest next call**, in the arm table: offer the
   record's last commitment; if it carries nothing, exhibit it and offer
   what the walk reaches behind it; if the record never carried this
   backing, open it in the next commitment. The door refusal and the commit
   refusal name all three.

**The vetoes.** A-D2 (incoherent floor; genesis-blind), A-D3 (weaker than
free; deletes a fault proof), A-D4 (bricks with no adversary) — each by at
least two angles independently. The branch's own two-call resume: vetoed
as ceremony (the first call proves an extension of nothing at a genesis
seat) whose one real content, identity against a venue-derived target, the
walk keeps in one call. Keeping the anchor arm beside the walk (the security
angle's SEC-1, with a no-progress refusal): vetoed by one-mechanism — its
only independent content was "seat here and do not serve", which is the
strand.

**What it costs, taken deliberately:**

- **The hostile-predecessor walk-back needs one exhibit per drop** the
  predecessor published after the last carrying state, from a party with the
  motive not to give them; the successor that cannot pay is STRANDED (the
  pre-existing bound: withholding is not a fault) rather than FRAMED (the
  branch's window: the supplier picks an older book and the honest key
  signs a provable rewrite). N is bounded by the lead time the rule-holder
  set, every drop is a provable fault, and CLAUDE.md's party rule sharpens
  to "keep every committed state from the last carrying one forward" for the
  operator's own replica — its own states, which it produced.
- **The exhibit is large today.** The practicality angle's spec proposal,
  recorded here as OWED and not built: frame each snapshot's NAME beside its
  digest in `stateRoot`, so a drop exhibit is the sorted (name, digest) list
  — 64 B per served backing, 19.2 MB → 6.5 kB. It changes a commitment root,
  so it is the spec's call; with it the walk's price disappears.
- **A witnessed own truncation is resumed onto in one call** (the security
  angle's F9, kept on purpose): publishing it was already a permanent
  stranger-checkable fault, and the honest fresh process serving the book
  the record stands on beats an honest process dark.
- **A superseded state re-offered on a live seat is a refusal, not a silent
  no-op** — which is what the two "no-op" tests should have asserted all
  along; they now re-offer the record's own last and assert `serves`.
- **The successor's "too old a state" test inverts**: the walk refuses it
  where the old door licensed it and relied on a fault proof after the fact.

**Residues recorded, candidate-independent, owed their own slices:**

- **The rule-holder erases one witnessed commitment per record, unprovably**
  (security's F4): a replacement effective AT the index of the incumbent's
  newest commitment puts that commitment in no term, so `lastCommitmentInForce`
  skips it, `termOf` returns undefined, every fault predicate returns false —
  and a final payment in it is gone and re-spendable, with the rule-holder
  (the backer by default, the party that owes the money) buying it for one
  record and a key it generates itself. The candidate mitigation is §C2's
  `effective > witnessed` (strictly later), which costs the zero-lead-time
  handover the 35d round allowed. A §C2 question; flagged to the maintainer.
- **A stranger stalls every COLD resume** (security's F3): `publishReplacement`
  refuses only bytes that do not decode, and `successionOf` filters junk with
  two strict Ed25519 verifies per record on every uncached walk — ~25 ms per
  junk record, ~900× at 4,000 records, against a restarting process whose
  cache is empty and whose commit timer the boot rule holds. The round's
  "flat" measurement was of the warm path. Cure: a rule-holder key pre-filter
  before verification, or memoising the filtered set per venue view.
- The sharded-key tail death (W3) and the boot rule: register every backing
  before the first commit, and hold the timer until every registered backing
  serves. Party rules, in CLAUDE.md.
- The genesis seat still cannot distinguish "never had" from "lost" — but the
  claim is now the operator's to sign, not the door's to guess.

**Spec change:** §C2's resume sentence ("a seat that already holds the pinned
book takes on this operator's own latest witnessed commitment") becomes the
walk's sentence, and the opening claim joins it; drafted on the paper repo,
the maintainer merges. The root-framing proposal is filed beside it.

**What HELD, and it is the centre of the slice: the raise arm is sound.** The

unpublished own-signed forgery (at or above the tip's sequence), a stranger's

## 2026-09-01 — Panel: the resume — the pin is a floor, the raise is from a held seat, and the pin the seat keeps is the book's own

**Status: the panel for F-A, and the design slice 36 builds.** Four angles
(security, inventory, simplicity/consolidation, practicality), all Opus, each
with its own probes and two with full prototype builds run against the suite.
The question as filed: may a takeover accept a later commitment by the seat's
OWN operator. The panel found the question undersized — F-A is one member of a
family — and converged on one design from four directions.

**What the angles found beyond the filing, each probe-proven:**

- The fault lands BEFORE the commit: the re-seated process double-signs a
  position it already receipted (`isDoublePosition`, unexcused — the era is
  genuinely recent, so the era door is open). The loss is the whole term, not
  a tail, and the receipt book's death opens a live double-spend window — the
  operator re-applies old signed requests and co-signs a different op at a
  spent nonce, breaking invariant 26's two promises exactly while the payee
  rule guarantees the replay material is in circulation.
- **The genesis twin (P6/G8), the majority configuration**: a restarted
  genesis process is seated by `register` unconditionally, `serves` reads
  true over the empty book, `takeOver` refuses at the genesis guard — no
  repair path exists in the API, and where E declares no replacementRule no
  succession can ever exist: a power cycle terminally bricks the backing with
  every balance zeroed in the operator's own voice.
- **The empty-pin wipe (P2)**: an heir that took the empty book and committed
  a real log — its fresh process is handed the empty book again with NO
  refusal. Fails open where F-A fails closed.
- **The never-carried wall (W1, independent of F-A)**: a backing registered
  after its operator's last commitment is unservable by every successor
  forever — the pin reads `dropped`, the empty book fires only on
  `undefined`, the evidence path needs a carrying state that never existed.
  One publication to enter, no adversary.
- The status-quo escape exists and costs a two-hop rotation through a
  throwaway key (a self-naming record revokes, never seats), the rule-holder
  live, and deliberate darkness throughout; the resume window closes at the
  restarted process's FIRST scheduled commit, so any runbook begins by
  holding the commit timer at boot.
- Bare option (a) — own-term acceptance with no anchor — is unsound twice
  over: it deletes the heir-may-not-license-its-own-commitment guard (the
  killer test the 35d round added on purpose), and it bounds the offer below
  while leaving it open ABOVE — whoever supplies the recovery input feeds the
  operator an OLDER own state and the whole F-A price returns,
  attacker-selected. The recurring shape, one rung up.
- A key THIEF gains nothing from any own-later acceptance: every capability
  the door could add already exists behind the signature; the door decides
  only what the honest implementation will serve.
- `dropping` is not an exit (it converts the shrink fault into a drop fault
  against the same key), and the `dropping` guard is process-scoped — a
  process that never registered a backing never scans it (W3, recorded).

**The vetoes.** Status quo was vetoed independently by three angles: it
prices a SEQUENTIALITY event (a power cycle, ordinary on the LoRa-class
hardware this project targets) at the CONCURRENCY rule's tariff ("one writer
at a time" is about two live writers, and a restarting writer never broke
it); its only advised path manufactures a permanent stranger-checkable fault
against an honest key; and the honest artefact is byte-identical to a key
thief's, so routine crash-shrinks would launder the guilty while accusing the
honest. Bare (a) was vetoed for the downgrade and the deleted guard.

**The design, one sentence per piece, each piece probed by at least one
angle:**

1. **The pin is a FLOOR; the raise is this operator's own latest witnessed
   commitment, licensed only from a seat already holding the pinned book**
   (the reSync gate — which makes the EXISTING fast-forward prefix loop the
   extension proof, at zero new mechanism: `lastCommitmentInForce` asked at
   its second bound, the identity check widened from one admissible value to
   two). Identity against the RAISED target is what refuses the downgrade
   and the unpublished forgery — the target is venue-derived, which is the
   venue check `committedLogFor` never had. Simplicity's table: zero new
   predicates, messages, arguments or helpers; suite green; the truncation
   guard stays green because the raise requires a held seat.
2. **The seat's stored pin becomes the identity of the commitment the BOOK
   stands on** — written by `takeOver` for what was taken, REWRITTEN by
   `commit` for what was published — and `serves` compares it against the
   record's unbounded answer. Detection everywhere: a process seated at the
   pin that stops reads serves-false and `awaitingTakeover` non-empty; the
   one-writer twin fails closed on the losing side (the superseded process
   stops serving); the double-spend window closes because a stale book
   cannot serve at all. This is the same self-detecting-staleness principle
   the seat and the pin already carry, applied to the last input left open.
3. **Registering is holding the book only where the record pins nothing** —
   for every seat, genesis included; the genesis refusal in `takeOver` is
   deleted, and a genesis process resumes by the same two calls as any other
   (the empty-book anchor, then the raise). One assertion and one refusal
   REMOVED.
4. **The never-carried wall closes through the existing evidence path**: an
   own latest commitment exhibited as `dropped` licenses the empty book,
   exactly as a predecessor's does.

**The uniform sentence, for the spec:** the book a seat serves is the one the
record's last commitment stands on; a fresh process resumes it from its own
latest witnessed commitment, from a seat holding what the handover pinned;
registering is holding the book only where the record pins nothing — for
every seat, genesis included.

**Residues recorded, not built:** the walk-back for one's OWN deliberately
dropped backing is not licensed (re-seat at the pin is that path) — [corrected 2026-09-02: the slice as BUILT licensed it, by moving the evidence path's reference to the record's last commitment, which is where the round's A1–A3 opened; the fix panel's walk licenses it soundly — see that entry];
`isRewrittenHistory` wants the walked-chain parameter if any door ever calls
it; a genesis seat cannot distinguish "never had this backing" from "lost its
book" (both resolve through the same exhibit, and the ambiguity is slice
13's recorded unreadability); the boot rule — hold the commit timer until
every registered backing serves — is a party rule, stated in CLAUDE.md.

## 2026-09-01 — The 35d round and its fix panel: the seat is a link and a provenance, and every refusal must leave a live path

**Question:** the review round owed by slice 35d's implementation. Five angles,
all Opus (the door adversarially, the replaced-mechanism inventory, the
seat/walk/cache reader, the tail/mark/idempotency seam, the test sweep with
mutations), every finding probe-proven and the blocking ones independently
re-verified by the session before being acted on. Four blocking findings —
all on one seam — then a five-angle fix panel (simplicity, security,
practicality, consolidation, inventory), each arguing the choice from its
lens with its own probes. The full finding set is in the round's working
notes; this entry records what was decided and why.

**The four blockers, and what the panel showed each really was:**

- **F1 — the seat was the walk's LAST link, not this operator's own.** A
  rotation queued at the heir's link is invisible until the heir's own force
  arrives (the walk stops at the first pending link), then locks the heir out
  for its ENTIRE term — the rule-holder writes the queued record's effective
  index, so the window is its choice. The locked-out heir's ordinary scheduled
  commit roots a drop that reads as rewritten history in BOTH argument orders,
  and that empty commitment becomes the pin for the next heir, breaking the
  one-argument takeover for every later rescuer. A crash-restart under a
  queued rotation triggers it with no adversary at all.
- **F2/F2b — an absent pinned target was a permanent wall, and the zero-width
  term is an eraser.** An operator dark before its first commitment left the
  backing unservable by every party forever. Worse: one record with
  `effective == incumbent.from` (legal under `effective >= at`) empties a term
  retroactively — the erased committed state places in no term, so
  `isRewrittenHistory` is false in both orders and the book cannot even
  accuse; and TODAY'S `>=` filter is itself a past-reading mover
  (`operatorAt` at an already-read index changes when the record lands),
  which is the hazard fault.ts forbids, purchasable for one publication.
- **F3 — the resumed operator's era window is a minting window.** Between its
  fast-forward and its first new-term commitment, its receipts name an era
  `eraLapsed` reads as ended — and `lapsed` is the excuse the fault pair
  reads, so its equivocation is unprovable while every liveness signal reads
  normal, the payee's freshness rule PASSES (the stale era IS its latest
  commitment), and the tokens never expire. 35c's recorded price ("for as
  long as the key stays dark") was false: the key is trading.
- **F4 — a stale BOOK under a current SEAT did not detect itself.** An heir
  that synced early in the lead time, whose predecessor then legitimately
  committed again, had no signal — its first commitment was a permanent
  shrink fault, both parties honest, and the customer-visible symptom is a
  wrong balance in the operator's own voice.

**The panel's synthesis — one design, five convergent voices:**

1. **The seat is this operator's OWN link** — the last link of
   `successionAhead` naming it — and it must be the link in force or the link
   pending; the genesis test becomes "my seat is the chain's first link"
   (the `ahead.length` proxy dies with the fix). Security ran five adversary
   shapes against it and nothing opens; a retired mid-chain key gains only a
   callable, inert refusal. The inventory traced every (old seat, new seat)
   pair through the tail disposition: no wrong branch — a live tail can only
   exist under an in-force-tip seat, which is exactly the re-sync branch.
2. **The walk voids a replacement whose effective index is not strictly
   later than its predecessor link's force — unless it names the incumbent**
   (a revocation-by-renaming at the boundary must still revoke; probed, the
   exemption is load-bearing). This is the walk's existing void rule set
   where its own comment already puts it ("two operators in force at one
   index"), it deletes a purchasable past-reading mover and the eraser class,
   and it costs exactly one test fixture that sat on the degenerate boundary.
   Recorded shape changes: a void record no longer spends its index's slot in
   the same-index tie, and readers on the two code versions can disagree
   about a past index of a chain containing a degenerate record (migration
   note; the record class is now unbuildable).
3. **Only behind that rule, an absent pin is the EMPTY book**: takeOver
   conforming to `lastCommitmentInForce`'s documented contract ("never
   publishing at all must not read as punctual"), generalizing register's own
   genesis rule — the book you take on is what the record pins before your
   seat, the empty one where it pins nothing. Fires ONLY on an undefined
   target, never the dropped-target case (which keeps its evidence path); the
   mark moves only where a commitment backs it. Alone this option was VETOED
   by two angles (it converts F2b's brick into a silent debt erasure, and an
   empty book resets nonces, framing honest holders); the ordering is the
   soundness argument, not a preference.
4. **The era condition joins `shut`**: one commit-first door, two conditions,
   two messages — an operator whose own current era already reads lapsed
   commits before it co-signs, which restores §C2's "takes effect only from
   the first index at which it has published its own commitment" as a door
   rule rather than a force rule. Placement is load-bearing: at the doors
   only, never in `caughtUp`/`adoptOne` — placed there, the return commit
   refuses ITSELF (probed: permanent self-inflicted lockout). `shut` sheds
   its dead `isInForce` copy (the `inForce` door runs first at both sites).
   Rejected: receipts naming their seat (a self-asserted field reproduces the
   bug; +18% wire on a LoRa-class payload; every held receipt stops
   verifying), and merging with `gapOpen` (two orthogonal windows, two
   audiences — probed in both directions). The adoption wrinkle is not a
   hole: `receiptStatus` reads the log before the era, so an adopted leg is
   `witnessed`. The reader-side residue — a payee accepting from a resumed,
   uncommitted operator — gets a party-rule sentence: a receipt whose era
   predates the operator's current seat is stale on its face, and the seat is
   readable from the chain.
5. **The seat gains the pin — possession WITH provenance**: the seat stores
   the taken commitment's identity, and `serves` compares the seat against
   the tip AND the stored pin against the recomputed one. The pin is frozen
   at the effective index (probed against a predecessor committing at every
   index of the lead time: one re-sync, ever), recomputed per call rather
   than cached (it can move at an unchanged cache key; 0.02ms flat on a
   walked chain), and rewritten on every re-sync. The identity is the
   commitment triple through ONE comparison, retiring three inline copies.
6. **`commit` refuses to silently drop an in-force backing — with a per-call
   acknowledgement** (`dropping`). Unconditional refusal was vetoed by
   security's hostage probe (one withheld backing silences a multi-backing
   operator's whole book and fires every grade); silent dropping was vetoed
   by inventory (it converts F4's shrink fault into a drop fault against the
   same honest party). The acknowledgement threads both: the forgetful
   operator gets a refusal naming takeOver, the besieged one names its choice
   and its healthy backings commit. `commit` reads its served set once and
   threads it (a pin-aware `serves` is uncached, so the old
   read-twice was a TOCTOU). A public reader lists the backings in force
   without the book.
7. **The walked chain is threaded through `gapOpen`, `gapLegsFor` and
   `eraLapsed`** (optional trailing parameter). Re-graded from "recorded
   design call" to part of the fix: the memo halved the crypto but left 3-4
   unmemoised walks per door call — ~4.9s per payment at 100 junk records,
   ~80ms with the chain threaded (60×), and junk costs the adversary one
   publication each. For the off-grid deployments this project cares about,
   compute IS the wire.

**Residues recorded, not fixed:**

- **The same-block amnesty** (security's probe, corrects 35c's price): a
