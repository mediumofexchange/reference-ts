## 2026-10-05 — Make the transparent profile lit notes under the pool's rules, with each output named by the statement that creates it (design slice, Next 10)

**Status:** accepted (design; reviewed). The specification change is committed. The lit construction's layouts
come next, before any code.

**Question:** the [direction](#2026-10-05--build-extensions-claim-layer-profiles-beside-the-shielded-pool-each-chosen-per-backing)
builds Extensions' claim-layer profiles beside the pool, each declared per backing in **E**, after a design slice. That
slice maps each profile against open entry, public supply verification, holder authorization, compartmentalized
failure, recovery and presentation. It decides what each profile shares with the pool's reader, wallet, journal and
commands, and how a closure that mixes profiles reports its metrics. It also maps Basis. Extensions defines the
transparent profile as "a per-backing public ledger of key-controlled balances", transferred "by signature at its next
nonce", with a challenge window at snapshot redemption. Construction's Appendix retired that ledger from the core
because "every rule in §C2b and §C3 then carried a second branch — a spend-sequence position standing in for a
nullifier, a demand's commitment resting on the lock alone, non-service read against a balance state, a challenge
window that binds a careless double-spender and never a deliberate one". Built as written, the profile brings those
branches back, now in the reference. What is the smallest construction for each profile?

**Decision:**
1. **The transparent profile is lit notes.** It keeps the pool's objects: notes, commitments, nullifiers, the spent
   set, the ordered statement trail, scopes and segments, commitments and directories, receipts, demands, acceptances,
   settlements, withdrawals, non-service requests and snapshot redemption. Two things change. Every statement
   publishes the openings of the notes it consumes and creates. Every act the pool authorizes by a proof is
   authorized by signatures of the keys that own the consumed notes. A note's owner is a holder's public signature
   key, generated by the receiver (invariant 25). Validity is the pool's per-backing conservation, checked in the
   clear from the openings, so a reader verifies signatures and sums instead of proofs. **Each output is named by the
   statement that creates it.** Its randomness is derived from that statement's consumed nullifiers and the output's
   position, or for an issuance from its K-authorized identity. So no other statement can create the same commitment.
   In the pool the randomness is private, which is what makes output uniqueness safe. Lit openings are public, so
   without this binding anyone who saw a statement before admission could submit another statement creating one of
   its outputs, at the cost of a zero-valued one, and admission's uniqueness check would refuse the original on every
   deterministic retry. The nullifier is computed from the public commitment, so double-spend prevention and the
   spent set work as in the pool. A demand names its notes by the same tags and is enforced by the same spent set
   (§C3). A non-service request is signed by the note's owner, in the shape of pool-recovery C2b.5.1. Snapshot
   redemption follows C2b.3a–c, so **the account ledger, its nonces and the challenge window retire** from Extensions
   to Construction's Appendix, with their cost: a payee who accepted an unwitnessed payment cannot redirect the payer's
   snapshot holding, as in the core. Two rules read differently and are named, not branched. C2b.5's request is the
   holder's act by its owner signature, not by a proof. C2b.7's targeted refusal is in scope for transfers, because a
   lit statement shows its keys.
2. **The lit layouts carry these requirements** into their own document, before code:
   - one canonical authorization encoding: strict Ed25519, one signature per input position, in a fixed order, so a
     statement has one signature hash;
   - fixed domain tags per kind and construction, because one key may be an owner, a presenter key and K;
   - every reader refuses an output whose randomness is not derived as item 1 states, so the binding is a validity
     rule and not a wallet convention. The issuance and spend derivations take distinct domain tags. An issuance's
     identity excludes its own output's commitment. Every statement that creates outputs either consumes a real input
     or is an issuance;
   - the derivation replaces pool-recovery C3.5's per-segment `rho_out` and its disclosure count, and pool-authority
     C2.10.8's settlement exception: a re-signed settlement keeps its output, since no one can create it first. A lit
     payment request names a key and a quantity, not an exact output (pool-delivery's prepared output);
   - a request's identity excludes its signature bytes;
   - an exact replay is the same statement bytes and returns the prior answer (invariant 26);
   - a terms layout of the lit construction's own, sharing the root-terms frame, name and signature. pool-v3 §11.2's
     clauses name `moe/pool/v3` and carry v3's silence fields, including a challenge window nothing now reads. The lit
     layout has no such field.
3. **Offline payment keeps its Extensions definition and its place in the direction's order.** The lit profile gives
   deferred submission and nothing more. A payee can carry the payer's signed statement to the sequencer later. The
   residual is the race for the note. The statement binds its segment, so a handover, a return or a lapse before
   submission voids it, and the payer must sign again. A second statement spending the same note is no evidence of
   fraud: an honest payer signs again after a segment change, a refusal or a fee change, and the key is a pseudonym
   the payer chose. Chaum, Fiat and Naor's identity-revealing note binds an identity at withdrawal. Over the shielded
   pool it is a new relation and construction version, inside the declared proof system. Its design is offline
   payment's own step.
4. **The accumulator's and the Chaumian profile's first steps.** The direction's order stands: both come after
   release, the accumulator first. The accumulator's stated need is a cheaper check than a conservation proof per
   statement. In v3's proof system, verification work and proof size grow only with the logarithm of the circuit's
   size, so the saving lies chiefly in proving, on the holder's device. Its design starts by measuring the spend
   relation's proving time against a membership-only relation's on the target devices. The Chaumian profile's needs
   are compatibility with existing mints (Cashu, Fedimint) and the cheapest build for a trusted circle that wants
   unlinkability without proving on its devices, which neither the pool nor lit notes serve. Its design starts by
   naming its compatibility target.
5. **What the profiles share.** A profile is one construction named in **E**, with its own versioned layouts. Its
   backings sit in scopes of that construction only (C1.2: a scope is per operator, construction domain and venue).
   Each such scope has its own segment, journal, commitments and directory. pool-authority C2.10.3 puts every backing of
   a scope in one directory over one segment state, and pool-v3 §7.1 excludes a checkpoint whose directory and scope
   are unequal. Shared are the venue and its record ranges, the root-terms frame, the journal's
   admission and commitment schedule, the receipt and recovery clocks, the evidence and replay stores and the `moe`
   commands. Each construction supplies its statement check (proof, or signatures and sums), its record and terms
   layouts and its wallet's holding scan. The commands dispatch on the construction named in a backing's terms. One
   `moe` role directory may hold backings of several constructions. An inventory of the runtime at `d81079b`
   (read only) places the seams:
   - **One validity seam.** Every mode (admission, replay, adoption, force) judges a record in `state.ts`'s one state
     machine. The proof is asked for in one place (`checkProof`, behind the `ProofCheck` interface). Issuance
     signatures are checked beside it, and acceptance and release signatures through its recovery check
     (`recovery.ts`, `authorization-evidence.ts`). A lit check of signatures and sums takes the proof's place. The
     anchor checks become "the input's commitment is an output and unspent".
   - **Layouts are per construction.** `records.ts` fixes each kind's public-input count, an exact authorization
     length (0, 64 or 136 bytes), the capsule count and a proof, non-empty except for a withdrawal's. A lit record
     has openings and owner signatures in place of a proof, so it needs its own tables. The history hash binds the note root
     (`commitments.ts` `nextHistoryHash`), and the v3 domain hashes the circuit identities (`configuration.ts`).
     Both are reasons the lit profile is a construction of its own, with its own domain.
   - **Sizes.** About 9,000 of the runtime's 21,000 lines serve a lit profile unchanged: the venue and Ergo code, the
     evidence and spent-set code, the service, terms and trail. About 2,100 are shielded only: circuits, prover and
     verifier, capsules, the note and scope trees, the seed scan. The rest is shared code with a seam of tens of lines
     per module, plus the wallet's proving and scanning paths (about 1,200–1,800 lines). Those counts are from reading
     and are estimates.
6. **Metrics over mixed closures.** pool-v3 §11.2 refuses reliance, so no current backing's closure has another
   member. Each backing reports its own construction with its supply figures: "proved" for the pool, "public" for the
   transparent profile, "asserted" for a Chaumian profile. Invariant 3's weakest-member qualifier is computed when a
   version takes up reliance, as the minimum over the closure and the denomination chain, fixed at signing.
7. **Basis** ([`basis.es`](https://github.com/BetterMoneyLabs/chaincash/blob/78475e30362571acf56e4e38276a9d6c0a84ce0c/contracts/offchain/basis.es))
   is not a profile of its own. Its parts map as follows:
   - Its debts, the cumulative amounts A owes B, are identified issuance by A.
   - Its tracker is the sequencer, and the AVL digest the tracker posts is a commitment.
   - Its novation needs the debtor's and the tracker's signatures to move a debt. The transparent profile moves
     claims by the holder's signature alone, so the profile subsumes novation.
   - Its occasionally connected trading runs through a local tracker: a sequencer whose venue is reachable only at
     times. That is witnessing over an intermittent venue (C2.9) and slice 12's transport, not offline payment.
   - Its redemption without the tracker's signature, more than three days of blocks after the tracker's last update,
     is its silence clause: the nearest analogue is C2b.6's snapshot redemption.
   - Its payment timestamps are wall-clock milliseconds. Here time is a witnessed index (C0b).
   - What Construction lacks is the reserve contract. It pays a holder from collateral against the tracker's committed
     state, without the obligor's live acceptance. In C3 terms that is an acceptance made by a venue script in place
     of K's signature, and "refusal becomes proof" for a chain-asset payout. It is recorded as a candidate Extension
     for after the transparent profile. It is not part of this design.
8. **Order:** the lit construction's versioned layouts (a `moe/lit/v1` document in the specification) before slice
   13, then its implementation through the shared seams; offline payment, the accumulator and the Chaumian profile
   after release, in the direction's order, each starting from item 3's or item 4's first step.

**Rationale:**
- *The account ledger, as Extensions has it:* balances and nonces need a second state model, demands committed by
  locks alone, non-service objects read against balances, and the challenge window. Those are the branches the
  Appendix records as the reason the ledger left the core. Lit notes need one changed check per act (signatures and
  sums in place of a proof), and every other rule is the pool's. The UTXO form also lets a holder take a fresh key
  for each output. Its trail stays public and linkable by the spend graph, so this is pseudonymity, not privacy. The
  account form makes clearing cycles easier to find from a balance per key. Lit notes show the same edges in the
  trail.
- *Reuse the pool's records with an empty proof:* pool-v3's records, configuration and identities are adopted bytes
  (§1). A record without a proof is not a v3 record, so the lit profile is its own construction with its own name.
  It reuses v3's layouts where they carry over, by reference.
- *No transparent profile:* this is the earlier course, with fewer mechanisms. The direction already chose against
  it. With lit notes the added mechanism is small: a signature check and an opening per note.
- *Outputs with free randomness, as in the pool:* this is simpler to state. But public openings would then let any
  observer front-run a statement's outputs (item 1). Forbidding zero-valued outputs would only raise the price of
  that attack, so the binding is in the commitment.
- *A double-spend evidence object for offline lit payment (the first draft):* honest re-signing after a segment
  change, a refusal or a fee change produces the same pair, so the object would accuse honest payers. No rule read
  it, and the key it names is the payer's chosen pseudonym. It was dropped.
- *Gating the accumulator and the Chaumian profile on a measured need (the first draft):* this would have amended
  the direction, and the Chaumian profile has a need neither the pool nor lit notes serve (item 4). The first steps
  stay; the order stands.
- *Costs:* the lit profile publishes every holding and transfer. An issuance's output names the receiving key, and
  under identified issuance the statement also names the recipient (C1.1). A refusal can be aimed at one key, and a
  non-service count of *m* distinct requests does not catch a refusal of one holder. Every backing that declares the profile accepts these costs in its terms, and invariant 3
  marks a closure that includes it. A lit statement is a few hundred bytes against the pool's 15 KB records, and a
  reader checks Ed25519 signatures instead of proofs. Both effects are estimated, not yet measured.
- *Falsifiers:*
  - a pool rule in C2b or C3 that lit notes cannot keep without a branch of its own;
  - a holder-authorization path the signatures miss, where a party other than the owner could move or present a note;
  - a measurement showing that lit replay is not materially cheaper than pool replay at the design point.

**Evidence:** Extensions' four profiles and offline payment; Construction C0, C1, C2b, C3 and the Appendix entries
on the transparent ledger and redirection; pool-v3 §§1–3; the [retirement case map](#2026-10-03--retire-the-pilot-and-the-transparent-path-against-a-case-map-of-their-checks-slice-10-m10e1);
Basis's contract at `78475e3`; the code inventory in item 5. No measurement. Limits: Basis was read from its contract's
own description and code, not from a running tracker.

One fresh adversarial review, of the first draft and its specification commit, found four majors, all resolved here:
- the offline double-spend object was unsound and read by no rule (dropped, item 3);
- public openings let anyone front-run an output (outputs are named by their statement, item 1);
- a shared terms codec and a directory mixing constructions contradicted adopted v3 (its own terms layout,
  item 2, and one directory per scope, item 5);
- gating two profiles amended the direction, and the Chaumian need was misstated (item 4).

Its minors were also taken:
- offline payment's text is scoped to its identity-revealing mechanism;
- the stale challenge-window mentions are noted (pool-recovery's is left: v3 reads that text as adopted);
- identified issuance is restated;
- the Appendix entry names C2b.5 and C2b.7;
- "new primitive" became "new relation";
- the proof system's logarithmic verification is stated;
- the layout requirements of item 2 were added;
- Basis's local tracker and silence clause were corrected.

Its read-back found the majors resolved and asked for the derivation's validity rules and its replacement of C3.5's
and C2.10.8's derivation in item 2, and three wording fixes. All were taken. The review confirmed holder authorization (no path lets a non-owner move, present, withdraw or lock a note). It also
confirmed that public tags only remove C1.5's aborted-presentation leak, that the window's retirement costs what is

depth, and venue-ergo's record ranges for lit record sizes.

## 2026-10-05 — Build Extensions' claim-layer profiles beside the shielded pool, each chosen per backing

**Status:** direction; the design slice that precedes code is not yet begun (WORK.md Next 10).

**Question:** the reference builds one claim layer, the shielded pool. Construction already makes the claim-layer
construction one field of a backing's E, with the transparent ledger, the per-denomination accumulator and Chaumian
blind signatures as Extensions profiles, and offline payment as an Extensions mechanism. Deployments with genuine
tradeoffs want weaker or no unlinkability for a cheaper check (no proofs, or very light ones), lit ledgers, or
transfer without reaching a sequencer (mesh transport, as Basis on Ergo does for reserve-backed credit). Should the
reference build only the pool?

**Decision:**
1. The reference builds Extensions' profiles beside the pool, each declared per backing in E, each a complete
   capability through the `moe` commands. The pool stays the core.
2. A design slice comes first, with no runtime code. It maps each profile against open entry, public supply
   verification, holder authorization, compartmentalized failure, recovery and presentation. It decides what the
   profiles share with the pool's reader, wallet, journal and commands, and how a closure that mixes profiles reports
   its metrics. It also maps Basis (reserve-backed bilateral credit with a tracker's committed tree) onto Construction:
   an instance of the transparent profile with offline payment, a profile of its own, or a transport. It is
   independently reviewed and clears C0a before dependent code.
3. Order, cheapest and most useful first: the transparent profile (no proofs, pseudonymous keys; its cases recovered
   from the retired path's case map at `8d207eb`); offline payment (with the Basis comparison and slice 12's
   transport); the accumulator; Chaumian, which gives up most (supply becomes the signer's assertion).
4. Proposed timing: the design slice after slice 11; the transparent profile before release assurance (slice 13), so
   the first release offers it; the others after release.

**Rationale:** the specification already prices each profile (Extensions: "The price"), so a deployment can choose
with the costs stated, and a lighter profile serves holders the pool cannot (phones, small circles, offline
exchange). Alternatives: the pool only (the earlier course: fewer mechanisms, but no lighter option for deployments
that do not need unlinkability); every profile at once (each is a claim layer with its own state, reader, wallet,
recovery and fault rules, so the review surface grows with each).

**Limits:** each profile adds code to review and secure, and delays release where it precedes it. A transparent
backing is pseudonymous only: every holding and transfer is public and linked, so one key tied to a person exposes
its history, and a refusal can be aimed at one key. Mixing a weaker profile into a closure weakens the metrics above
it (Extensions, the Chaumian profile). AGENTS.md's direction paragraph still names one production path and the
retired transparent path; it is to be updated to this direction.

**Spec change:** none for the direction: Construction already declares the claim layer per backing and Extensions
defines the profiles. Each profile the reference builds needs its own versioned layouts, specified before its code.

## 2026-10-05 — Record a kept file's digest from the pages its keep point changed (slice 11 M11b12, Next 4(v))

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** pool-v3 §14 lets a reader resume kept state only after "the whole kept state must match one SHA256
digest held in storage only the reader writes". The reference recorded that digest as SHA256 of the whole kept file
at each keep point that changed a row (storage decision, 2026-09-29, "The file"). A read at a new venue index
changes kept answers, so every block's first read hashed the whole file. For the operator, whose journal reads its own
checkpoint before admitting, this added about 2.8 ms per 10³ statements to the first admission after each block
(M11c2: 91 ms at 10⁴, 343 ms at 10⁵), so about 2–3 s at the design point against the 1 s admission budget. A reader's
or wallet's steady-state read pays the same once per block it reads.

**Decision:**
1. The digest is the root of a SHA256 tree over the file's pages. Each page's SHA256 covers its bytes; a short last
   page is hashed as it lies. Each group of 256 consecutive page hashes is hashed under `v3-kept-page-group`. The
   root is SHA256 over `v3-kept-file`, the page size and page count (u64 each) and the group hashes. It is still
   one SHA256 digest that every byte of the file and its length reach.
2. A kept file runs in SQLite's write-ahead-log mode with automatic checkpoints off. At each keep point the store
   reads the page numbers from the log's frame headers (the frames carrying the header's salts: every page written
   since the log was last reset, a superset of those changed). It moves the log into the file
   (`wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`), rehashes those pages and any past the previous length from the file, and records
   the root. A log that another connection keeps from being moved in whole refuses as the file in use.
3. Opening is as before in strength. The store first opens the file, which recovers what the log committed and drops
   what it did not, and moves the log in. A log it cannot move in refuses as the file in use. Then it hashes every page
   of the closed file and compares the root with the recorded digest. The page hashes it computes seed the
   store. A mismatch discards the file, its log and the digest, the log first.
4. Layout 10: a file of an earlier layout is discarded once. A file whose SQLite cannot keep a log is refused, since
   every later opening would discard it.

**Rationale:** opening recomputes the root from the file's bytes, so the incremental hashes never vouch for anything
the next check does not see. An incremental hash list that missed a page would make the next opening discard
the file and replay in full, a cost and never a wrong reuse. A crash keeps the old order: between commit and the recorded
digest, the log's committed frames are recovered on opening and the file no longer matches, so it is discarded. Inside a walk
the file changes only at keep points, so a copy taken mid-walk opens at its last keep point. Alternatives:
- *the rows that change at every index kept in a file of their own:* a block's read also classifies the new
  checkpoint and replays its records, so the main file changes at each block too;
- *recording the digest less often:* a crash between recordings discards the whole file, a full replay (hours at
  the design point);
- *the rollback journal's page list:* read before commit from a journal SQLite writes for its own recovery, with
  pages appended past the old length unrecorded. The log's frames name every page written, in SQLite's documented
  format;
- *a hash of rows rather than pages:* it needs a keyed tree of rows kept beside them, more mechanism than the log
  SQLite already writes;
- *hosting the read state in the operator's journal database*, which has no digest: it sidesteps §14's check rather
  than meeting it, and readers and wallets would keep the cost.

**Evidence:** a replay-store test checks the recorded digest against the full-file digest at every keep point
through growth past one group, freed pages from collected namespaces, reopening, and a commit left in the log by a
stopped process (discarded). The kept-file tests and the wallet's and journal's abrupt-exit drills pass with the
digest taken from the closed file; a second store meets the first's write lock at its opening or its walk. A
model of the mechanism (scratch) found no mismatch between incremental and full roots over 1,223 commits to 1 GB with
savepoint rollbacks, updates and deletes. Probe ([measurements](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#a-kept-files-digest-from-its-changed-pages-m11b12)):
a block's read closes in about 23 ms at 66 MB and at 1 GB, where the whole-file hash took 0.19 and 2.9 s.
One fresh adversarial review found nothing blocking and confirmed the safety claim on every path it built. Its stress
run of 3,337 keep points (random intervals, cache spill, savepoint rollbacks, collections and writes outside walks,
reopenings) found no mismatch. Taken:
- *material:* a keep point whose log another connection kept from being moved in threw before the data-version check,
  so the walk was not marked lost and its close committed what it wrote after; such a keep point now loses the walk;
- *minor:* the pages such a keep point's log named were dropped, and once the log restarted under new salts the next
  digest missed them (every later opening discarded); they now stay pending until a keep point hashes them;
- *hardening:* a digest path equal to the file's log, shared-memory or journal path is refused.
Each fix has a regression test that fails without it. A read-back found the fixes clean and checked their variants: a close
that fails after its commit, a discard, an in-process reopening, and nothing committed being rolled back. Its suggestion
was taken too: any failure to record the digest at a keep point, not only another connection's, loses the walk. Recorded,
not changed: the window between the opening check's hash and the store's own connection (below), and a digest path naming
the file under another spelling, which only discards.

**Limits:** opening still hashes every page, about 1.2 times one whole-file hash, so a restart at the design point
spends a few seconds there, once. The page hashes take 32 bytes per 4 KiB page in memory, about 18 MB for the
operator's read file at the design point. A deep history's records change more pages each than the probe's
shallow tables; M11c3 measures the operator's admission at depth. The log adds two files beside the kept file while
it is open, and a walk's log grows with what it writes between keep points. As before this change, opening checks the
closed file and only then opens the store's connection, holding no lock between, so a process that commits in that gap
is read from before a later opening discards the file. Kept files are each party's own, opened once (storage decision);
WORK.md Next 4 carries the review's proposed check under the store's own connection. The log parse reads each frame
header with its own system call, which a long first sync's keep points pay once per frame.

**Spec change:** none needed: §14 requires one SHA256 digest of the whole kept state held where only the reader
writes, and leaves how it is computed to the implementation.

## 2026-10-05 — Sync a command's view in bounded passes until it is caught up (slice 11 M11b11)

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** each `moe` command synced its directory's Ergo view once. One `ErgoVenue.sync` adds at most
`headersPerSupplier` (2,000) headers a supplier and reads at most `sectionBytesPerSync` (32 MiB) of sections, so a
view further behind stopped short of the tip and the command answered at that clock. M11c2's fresh reader at 10⁵
statements (about 7,300 blocks) judged at index 997
([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#the-runtime-at-depth-m11c2)); at the design point (about 788,000 blocks) a
fresh reader or wallet would need hundreds of runs, and an operator back from a long outage would serve from a
stale clock until it had been restarted enough times.

**Decision:**
1. `syncCaughtUp(venue, suppliers, onPass?)` (`ergo.ts`) repeats `sync` while a pass ended on progress that cost its
   supplier: a supplier stopped by its `header budget` (it added that many new headers), or
   `unresolvedReason: "section budget"` after at least one section was read. A `fetch budget` stop ends it. It
   returns the last pass's report with the number of passes.
2. `openView` (`cli/venue.ts`) syncs through it, so every command's sync catches up: operator open and each `serve`
   tick, relay publish and its wait, reader reads, wallet reads. Each pass that another follows is a `syncing`
   event on stderr (witnessed index, tip height).
3. `ErgoVenue.sync` and its budgets are unchanged: each pass is bounded and committed as one, so an interrupted
   catch-up keeps what its passes read, and the clock's rules hold at every pass.

**Rationale:** the loop ends because each pass that leads to another made progress that its supplier paid for. A
header budget on the best chain is a budget of headers with real work (under the mainnet and testnet rules; the
testnet's low hashrate makes them cheap there), bounded by the gap to the tip, which a pass closes far
faster than the network adds blocks; off it, the budget is charged to that supplier's side-branch quota
(forgiven only by the best chain's own advance), so a quota of 20,000 lasts about ten passes, plus one per 32,000
heights the view's best chain advances, its own catch-up included. A section-budget
pass advances the clock. A fetch-budget stop is free: a supplier claims a far tip and re-serves known headers. Any
pass that stops for a reason a retry cannot cure (a missing section once headers stop, the retained budget, a
failing or withholding supplier) ends it. Alternatives: raise the per-sync budgets (one pass then holds more headers and
section bytes in memory before its commit, and the design point still exceeds any fixed budget); loop only while
the clock advances (a pass can add headers without moving the clock, as when a supplier's budget stops it on a
heavier fork, and the next pass then moves it); loop in each command (seven call sites for one rule); loop inside
`sync` (one commit per call is its contract, and the library's callers choose their own cadence).

**Evidence:** tests (`ergo-venue`): a view 40 blocks behind under a 5-header budget reaches the tip in one call of
eight passes, each non-final pass heard, and a second call takes one pass; a catch-up reads past the section
budget to the tip; a retained budget ends it after one pass; a withheld section holds the clock while headers
arrive and ends when they stop; beside a supplier serving a longer side branch it ends once that quota is spent;
beside a supplier padding to its fetch budget it takes one pass, both while another supplier brings a new block
each pass and while the padder adds one side header each pass. One fresh adversarial review found the first rule
blocking: it also continued on a fetch-budget stop whenever any supplier made progress, so a padding supplier held
a command for as long as the chain grew (and, on a still chain, for one pass per header of its side quota, each
costing the reader a fetch budget of known headers). Its suggested rule is the one above; its two reproducers are
the last two tests, which hang under the first rule. Its read-back of the fix found nothing blocking; it checked

Command probe ([below](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#a-commands-view-caught-up-m11b11)): a view created on the

**Limits:** a catch-up's time is the header and section work it does, at about 13–15 ms a synthetic block here,

## 2026-10-04 — Fail a payment whose output another statement created, and fail no act with a pending receipt by the door's times (audit, area 29)

**Status:** accepted. This is the rolling audit of the v3 wallet (`wallet-store.ts`, `holdings.ts`,
`redemption.ts`, `wallet-request.ts`, `wallet-backup.ts` and the custody handling in `cli/wallet.ts`).
No specification change.

**Question:** do the wallet's custody, holder authorization and local accounting hold under
crashes, concurrent handles, stale or hostile views and restored state? This was checked against
pool-fees C1.2.3–5, pool-recovery C3.3–C3.8, pool-delivery C4.1–7 and pool-v3 §14. The audit found
two accounting defects:
- A payment one of whose outputs another statement created first stayed `prepared` for good, with
  its input reserved. The payee or a fee recipient can do this by handing one request to two payers
  or by paying itself. The operator refuses the record, and every reproof keeps the same outputs.
  The wallet already failed an issue or a settlement on the same evidence, but not a payment.
- A demand or settlement whose operator receipt the wallet held was marked `failed` when the venue
  passed the demand's C3.3 window or the acceptance deadline before the checkpoint carrying it.
  While it read `failed`, the same-count settlement guard (C3.5) let a second settlement be
  prepared at that disclosure count.

**Decision:**
1. `paid` fails a payment when one of its outputs exists (the canonical state or a forced effect)
   but not all four are in canonical history. A statement's outputs enter history together: replay
   reads by event position, and a successor imports whole statements. Every door refuses an output
   that already exists. So no record of that payment can be admitted, and its inputs are freed.
2. The time rules in `resolutions` (C3.3's window closed for a demand, `at > instant + 2·lag`, and a
   settlement's acceptance deadline passed) fail no act whose operator receipt is still pending. C3.8
   makes them the door's conditions, and a replayer does not re-judge an admitted statement's instant
   or deadlines. A receipt is pending while the canonical checkpoint's position is below the
   receipt's and the read proves no silence boundary. A checkpoint at or past that position without
   the statement contradicts the receipt (C2.10.9b). A boundary lapses it, since the unwitnessed
   tail is discarded (C2b.4.1). In either case the times judge the act again. Any act still fails
   if its segment ends, an input is spent otherwise, its demand stops standing, or its output
   exists. Acts are never reproved, so a saved receipt always names the act's current statement.
3. Mechanical changes:
   - `prepare`, `reprove` and `fulfill` read the caller's terms once (closes Next 4(f)).
   - One `savedPayment` check replaces two duplicated closures.
   - A restore refused for its snapshot profile wipes the seed it decoded.

**Rationale:** each rule is the evidence that already decides issues and settlements, applied to
the payment. Item 2 relies on the receipt's position and the reader's silence clock, which the
wallet already reads. So a receipted act stays `prepared` exactly as long as its admission is
still pending. Other options for item 2:
- Exempting every receipted act. The patch review rejected this: an operator that receipts and
  then goes silent, or checkpoints without the statement, froze the holder's notes in its own
  wallet and blocked C2b.3 recovery in a gap.
- Only making `pendingSettlement` count failed settlements that hold a receipt. This closes the
  guard but still reports an admitted act as failed.

**Evidence:**
- Regression tests, each failing before its fix:
  - `pool-v3-payer.test.ts`: a payment whose output another statement made;
  - `pool-v3-redemption.test.ts`, a pending receipt: a demand and a settlement past their times;
  - `pool-v3-redemption.test.ts`, a receipt lapsed by a silence boundary;
  - `pool-v3-redemption.test.ts`, a receipt contradicted by a checkpoint at its position. This one
    writes the row directly, since no fixture operator contradicts its receipts.
- The audit's independent review found item 1 (major) and item 2 (minor). Each was reproduced with
  stand-in proofs before the fix.
- Reviewed and found sound:
  - reservations across concurrent calls and handles, re-checked under `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` behind the
    owner fence;
  - crash and retry at every step (nothing is sent before the save; exact retries and resubmits
    send identical bytes);
  - a spend only of notes whose kept mark the output recovers;
  - the freeze on export;
  - the strict backup envelope and snapshot codecs, and the shape checks on restored rows;
  - no secret in any error;
  - owner-only file modes in the CLI;
  - time only from the witnessed index.
- Deferred (WORK.md Next 4):
  - (y) A restored handoff starts its `seen` watermark at zero, so the restored copy can build from
    a view older than its source synced at. This costs refusals, not funds. The lever is to carry
    `seen` at the next wallet profile change.
  - (z) pool-delivery C4.7's "a venue-created output awaiting adoption is reported as such" is not
    met. The wallet does not report such an output at all, rather than reporting it as spendable.
- Patch review (one fresh reviewer): `paid` and the refactors are sound. One major finding: the
  first form of item 2 exempted every receipted act; it now holds only for a pending receipt. The
  read-back of that fix found no new blocker:
  - `clock.boundary` is set exactly when the gap is open at the read's own index, never from the
    horizon. C2b.4.1 lets no predicted gap lapse a receipt, so for up to `lag` indices a new act
    is already routed to the venue while the receipted one still holds its notes.
  - Receipt and checkpoint positions are both in the receipt's own segment namespace.
- Two minor cases stay as they are:
  - A receipt under terms without silence whose term ends with no successor stays pending until a
    takeover segment fails the act as dead. Without silence the holder has no venue route then.
  - An operator that keeps committing without reaching the receipt's position is censoring
    (C2b.5 non-service), not something this patch introduces.
- Nits:
  - An exact `prepare` retry now copies the caller's terms before answering, as `freshen`, `issue`
    and `demand` do.
  - The tests do not cover a stale view against the output rule. The `judged` guard is unchanged.

**Spec change:** none needed.

## 2026-10-04 — Keep each witnessed note's tag with its mark, so a wallet's read hashes none (slice 11 M11b8)

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** M11c2's runtime probe at depth found a wallet's read growing with the notes it holds: a sync with
nothing new took 351 ms at 315 holdings and 984 ms at 1,065, against a flat 85–89 ms for a reader of the same
history ([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#a-wallets-tags-kept-with-its-marks-m11b8)). `holdingsOf`, `presentedBy` and
`demandsOf` each computed a note's tag `tagOf(nf)` (C3.3, the hash a demand locks a note by; a JavaScript Poseidon2
hash of 0.21 ms), three per unspent note at every read, and settle's lookup once more per note. With the per-note
lookups that is about 0.85 ms a holding. A shop holding 10⁵ unspent notes would spend about 85 s a sync, so about
seven syncs a day would use the steady-state budget of 10 CPU-minutes.

**Decision:**
1. **The mark keeps the tag.** The witness predicate appends `tagOf(nf)` to the opening it keeps (136 bytes: the
   opening's 104, then the tag), hashed once when the replay marks the output. `ownedNotes` reads it as
   `OwnedNote.tag`.
2. **Reads use the kept tag; what the wallet signs does not.** Holdings statuses, presented sets, standing demands
   and settle's search for the demanded notes use `note.tag`. A demand's tags, the lock checks on a payment's,
   fulfillment's or reproof's inputs, and settle's input order still hash the nullifier.
3. **A wrong tag is kept state to discard.** Completing a note to spend (`inputOf`) refuses, as `KeptStateMismatch`,
   a mark whose tag is not its nullifier's, as it refuses a wrong opening or nullifier. Where settle misses a
   demanded note by its kept tag and some kept tag is not its nullifier's, that is the same refusal, so the read
   replays rather than refusing `ABSENT` on every retry.
4. Kept replay layout 9: earlier kept files, readers' included, are discarded and replayed once. The witness
   table's columns are unchanged, so the operator journal's profile stays.

**Alternatives:** hash each note's tag once per read (one hash instead of three; still linear in holdings at
0.21 ms); ask the store for every demand's tags once and test notes against that set (still needs each note's tag);
skip the tags when the backing has no demand (any one demand in history, ended ones included, disables it).

**Evidence:** regression tests: each note read from its mark carries its nullifier's tag; a mark whose tag
alone is altered reads as held and is refused with the mismatch's message when completed (`pool-v3-wallet-kept`);
settle over a damaged tag on the demanded note and over another note's tag forged equal to it spends exactly the
demanded nullifier (`pool-v3-redemption`; fails with the miss guard removed); the hand-built marks of
`pool-v3-kept-state` carry the tag. Measured ([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#a-wallets-tags-kept-with-its-marks-m11b8)):
a read with nothing new at 1,065 holdings took 984 ms before and 235 ms after, about 0.84 and 0.14 ms a holding
beyond a reader's. One fresh adversarial review found nothing blocking: no path builds or signs a statement over a
wrong note or a kept tag, and an old-layout file is discarded. Its liveness finding (a damaged tag on the
demanded note made settle refuse `ABSENT` on every retry without discarding) is fixed by decision 3; its test
finding (the refusal asserted by class, no settle case) by the tests above. Its read-back found nothing blocking.

**Limits:** a holdings status read from a damaged tag (a locked note shown available, or the reverse) is advisory
until a spend completes the note, as M11b7's limits state for the rest of the mark. A demanded note whose mark
has a damaged backing or nullifier while every tag matches still leaves settle refusing `ABSENT` without a
discard, as before this change (a liveness limit; refusing a first miss as kept state would cost a replay on a
genuine miss). A read still visits each
unspent note's witness row and makes its per-note lookups, so it still grows with the notes held, at a smaller
rate.

**Spec change:** none needed: kept state is a local cache (pool-v3 §14).

## 2026-10-04 — Keep each witnessed output's nullifier and opening with its witness, and leave spent ones out of a wallet's read (slice 11 M11b7, Next 4(x))

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** M11c1 found that every wallet read (`ownedNotes`, then `holdingsOf`) recovered every output its
seed had ever received from its capsule again, spent outputs included: three JavaScript Poseidon2 hashes and two
HMAC derivations an output, about 1.2–1.7 ms. A shop receiving 100 payments a day would hold about 10⁵ outputs
after three years, and each read would take about 2 minutes, so ten syncs a day would exceed the steady-state
budget ([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#the-commands-over-a-thousand-statements-m11c1)).

**Decision:**
1. **The witness predicate marks what it chose.** It returns, for an output it witnesses, a mark: the nullifier
   that spends the output and its own bytes, here the note's opening (backing, owner, rho, value; 104 bytes).
   The replay keeps the mark in the output's witness row. The spend secret is not kept: the wallet's replay file
   stays without a secret, so losing or leaking it still costs no funds.
2. **A read leaves spent outputs out in the store.** `ReplayStore.unspentWitnessed` returns the witnessed outputs
   visible at (namespace, position) whose kept nullifier is not visible there, imports included, reading the
   witness rows rather than every output. The wallet's force overlay still removes what a force publication spent.
3. **A note is completed only to be spent.** `ownedNotes` reads each note's opening and nullifier from its mark.
   Its spend secret is recovered from its output when first read, and its anchor and path are read from the
   state then. The wallet completes the notes it selects (`inputOf`) inside the read that found them. A mark that
   does not decode, or whose output recovers another commitment, nullifier or opening, is kept state that does not
   match (§14): the wallet discards its kept state and reads once more. One path per namespace is read during
   `ownedNotes`, so a kept witness past the read position still refuses the read itself, as before.
4. Kept replay layout 8 (earlier files are discarded and replayed); the operator journal, which hosts the replay
   tables, becomes `pool-store/v3/6` (earlier journals refuse to open, as at every profile change; an operator's
   witness table is always empty, but no journal exists outside tests and drills, so no migration is written).
   A predicate returning anything but a mark or undefined is a caller error (TypeError).

**Alternatives:** keep the spend secret in the mark too (no recovery at spend time, but the replay file would hold
spending keys outside the database's backup and protection); keep openings in the wallet database instead (the
replay file is the per-output state and is not backed up, while the database is, so every backup would grow with
received outputs); delete a witness row once its output is spent (saves its later sibling rewrites, but a spend
in a successor segment cannot remove a row another fork's segment still imports, and the read already leaves it
out); recover only the outputs new since the last read (needs a per-read cursor beside the witnesses for the
same result).

**Evidence:** regression tests: the store leaves out an output once its kept nullifier is visible, in the
namespace and through an import, and keeps the mark's bytes (`pool-v3-replay-store`); kept and fresh reads give
the same notes, each equal to a scan of its output with the same secret, and a mark whose opening the output does
not recover (another rho or nullifier) is read as held but refused as kept state when completed; a damaged mark
under a re-recorded digest shows its value until a payment selects it, when the wallet replays and refuses
`FUNDS` (`pool-v3-wallet-kept`); `ownedNotes` refuses a read below a kept witness's tip (`pool-v3-kept-state`,
which fails with the eager path read removed). Measured
([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#a-wallet-read-from-kept-marks-m11b7)): 1.6 ms an output a read before,
0.01–0.02 ms after, at 2·10³–10⁵ outputs. One fresh adversarial review: its blocking finding (a succession
check script still passing a boolean predicate, which the replay then failed on) and its findings on a damaged
mark refused under no wallet code without discarding, untested eager path and nullifier checks, a query reading
every output (57–95 ms at 10⁵ outputs with 10 witnessed, 0.24 ms after), the docs, and the mark's layout version
are fixed above; the journal profile is accepted as stated. Its read-back of the fixes found nothing blocking:
a second pass of a read's callback after a discard repeats no write, every input is completed inside its read,
and the mismatch is raised only where the wallet discards; its suggested test of a predicate returning anything
else is added (`pool-v3-kept-state`).

**Limits:** the mark is local kept state: the digest check (§14) guards it against damage, and a mark changed
under a re-recorded digest shows a wrong value, or a spent note as held, until a spend completes it. Before, a
damaged capsule row dropped a note rather than inflating one. A discard meeting another connection's lock on the
kept file surfaces SQLite's busy error rather than `STORAGE`, as it did before this change. A read still visits each
unspent note's witness row, so it grows with the notes held, at a small fraction of the former rate. The replay
file now also shows each of the seed's notes' values and nullifiers, so a copy of it links every later public
spend of them, exact-amount spends included; it needs the database's protection as before.

**Spec change:** none needed: kept state is a local cache (pool-v3 §14), and a holder's own evidence includes its
note openings.

## 2026-10-04 — Serve evidence without the journal's turn, and refuse the journal's own damage by name (slice 11 M11b5, Next 4(s))

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** WORK.md Next 4(s) held six findings of the 2026-09-30 code review. The journal skipped an older own
segment's lost trail when serving, so it served a package a reader refuses instead of naming its damage. Each
wallet `GET /evidence` took the journal's turn (`run`), loaded its state and kept every venue answer of the active
scope in a write transaction: a command met BUSY, and `moe operator serve` queued every holder's sync behind the
schedule's commands. `adopt` let a ReplayRefusal escape unnamed. `client.package()` documented its bound as
`maxBytes` while holding about three times that. A walk's failed close replaced a read's refusal. The prover proved
under any domain, and the exported walks trusted `selection.domain`.

**Decision:**
1. **Serving takes no journal turn.** `serve` reads signed rows, which a command changes only inside one
   synchronous transaction, so it neither waits for a command nor makes one BUSY, and `moe operator serve` lets it
   pass the schedule's queue (the Ergo view publishes each snapshot whole, so reads during a sync answer from the
   previous one). It writes only where the venue's clock has passed the index this key's held commitments are kept
   through: then it keeps them through the clock under the fence, with any conflict their windows show, as a
   command's view does. Otherwise it reads the fence, identity and held commitments in one read snapshot. The
   selection rule (C2.4.3–4) is unchanged; the scope's term chains and revocations are a command's to keep.
2. **Serving refuses the journal's own damage by name.** Serving loads no admission state; what it serves is
   checked as it is read. The latest signed row must be the log's latest signing reply, byte for byte, before any
   answer is kept (as reopening checks); a held commitment of this key without its row, with no conflict recorded,
   is damage; a gap in the signed sequences, an own snapshot without its trail, or an own snapshot that does not
   decode is STORAGE. A held commitment shown by a newly read window whose own row no longer verifies as this
   key's, or holds another sequence's commitment, is STORAGE too, never a conflict (for commands as well). Only a snapshot taken from takeover evidence is
   served without a trail.
3. `adopt` names a block the opening's state refuses: REFUSED with its check, keeping nothing of it.
4. The whole-package helpers (`wholePackage`, `client.package()`) state their peak of about three times the
   package. Lowering it would need a second package encoder beside `encodeEvidencePackage`, whose defensive copy
   sits at the trust boundary; parties that keep evidence take parts into a store one at a time.
5. A read's failure stands over a failed cleanup (a walk's close, a package read's release or close), which goes
   with it as its `cause`; after a read that succeeded, a failed cleanup is reported.
6. The prover refuses a task naming another configuration before any witness; `classifyScopes`,
   `classifyScopeFrontier` and `replayTrail` refuse a selection under another domain (CONFIGURATION).

**Alternatives:** keep serving in `run` but without the scope's chains (still a turn and a write per GET); serve
from the kept answers as they stand, never writing (a selection up to a poll behind, and stale on a journal no
command drives); load the engine in `serve` on first use (a serve that sets memory could leave an in-flight
command's later transaction judging against a reloaded revision); drop `client.package()` (its harness callers
compare whole packages).

**Evidence:** a regression test per finding, each failing on the code before it (`pool-v3-store`,
`pool-v3-recovery-store`, `pool-v3-import-work`; the prover's in `store-check.mjs`); a serve answers while a
submission verifies and while another handle holds the database's write lock. One fresh adversarial review and a
read-backs; their findings (an older selection served over a lost or changed row, a damaged row read as a
conflict, a non-atomic fence read, a dropped cleanup failure, `withEvidence`'s `finally`, a row holding another sequence's commitment) are fixed above.

**Limits:** a serve finds the latest signing reply by scanning the log back over the admissions since the last
signing, as reopening does; it grows with admissions per checkpoint. The reopen checks of the admission state run
at a command, not at a serve.

## 2026-10-04 — Resume a kept walk, so a later read judges only new checkpoints (slice 11 M11b4, Next 4(k))

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.
**Spec change:** [pool-v3 §14 at dc51baf](https://github.com/mediumofexchange/money-from-first-principles/blob/dc51baf6a3cc46f548fdeba01291c64bf4d36c16/pool-v3.md#14-replay-retention-and-resource-bounds)

**Question:** WORK.md Next 4(k): under a silence or non-service clause the operator's journal reads its own
canonical checkpoint through the public reader before every admission and commit. Each read opened a new walk
and judged every held checkpoint again: a kept class (decision 2026-09, M5b.4a) skips only the replay, and the
directory, scope, terms signature, faults, lapse, clocks, imports and base all run again. The silence clock
started again from the segment's opening, every publication was walked, the held commitments around the
selection were walked, and the kept replay file was hashed whole after every read. A throwaway vitest probe
(stand-in proofs, fixture venue, one backing with a silence clause, empty checkpoints committed and published,
then `submit` of an issue timed) measured about 14 ms per held checkpoint per admission
([probe](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#an-admission-under-a-silence-clause-m11b4)). At one checkpoint per
witness interval for three years that is minutes per admission, against "admission ≤ 1 s per statement at
peak". A wallet's syncs and any reader with a kept file pay the same cost. How can a read rest on what earlier
reads classified without re-judging it, and still give a fresh read's verdict?

**Rule and conflict:** pool-v3 §14 "Kept classes" requires three checks "before reusing a kept class". A read
that relies on every older class (to find the latest valid checkpoint and the clock's boundary) would check every
class on every read, which undoes the purpose §14's own C0a text gives kept classes: "Kept classes replace
reclassifying every held checkpoint on each read."

**Decision:**
1. **A kept walk per selected backing.** Beside the kept classes, the kept replay store keeps (layout 7):
   - each backing's cursor: the term by its position and link, and the last sequence classified in it. The
     cursor never passes the last term, which stays open as the record grows; a cursor whose term is another
     link in this read's chain is kept state to discard;
   - each backing's valid candidates and the classes the walk read;
   - each silence clock's fold (last index read, boundary if found), written only when breakpoints were read
     and never behind a further one;
   - each backing's last classified publication position;
   - the judging index it read through, and the retained evidence's identity and lineage mark.
2. **When a read resumes it.** A read resumes the kept walk of its selected backing where the evidence store is
   retained (a file or a host's database), the read carries no receipt and no fault items of its own, and its
   judging index is at or after the kept one and not past the venue's witnessed index. The kept index is raised in the walk's transaction before any
   row, so a refused or interrupted read keeps nothing past it. A read at an earlier index, or from a private
   in-memory evidence store, uses a temporary walk and leaves the kept walk as it was. Another retained store's
   identity, or a lineage mark the store no longer holds (a copy restored from before the kept walk's last read),
   begins the kept walk again. The mark moves to the read's at any keep point or close of a resumed read that
   changed a row.
3. **What a resumed read checks.** It judges only the checkpoints and publications after the kept positions,
   fully as before. A class an earlier read made is checked where the read loads it (a canonical checkpoint, a
   predecessor, an opening, a publication's snapshot): the venue holds the same commitment at the same index;
   the kept snapshot authenticates against the retained directory and scope; a valid class's trail is retained
   and its state passes §14's snapshot check. Evidence no longer retained leaves the read unresolved, as a read
   of every checkpoint would; any other mismatch is kept state to discard (`KeptStateMismatch`, read again).
   Valid classes also check every sibling snapshot, and the clock's last snapshot is rechecked too. A kept-walk
   row that does not decode, or a candidate whose class is missing, is kept state to discard.
4. **`carrying` lists the selected backing's own carrying checkpoints** within its terms through the judging
   index. Every read classifies each of them, so the listing does not depend on which earlier reads of a kept
   walk classified what. Classes a read reaches only as dependencies of other backings (an import's predecessor,
   another backing's opening, every scoped backing's clock around the canonical checkpoint) are still classified,
   and no longer listed: a fresh read's listing changes where a dependency of another backing was listed before
   (three test expectations).
5. **Lineage.** The evidence store keeps an identity and one lineage row per batch (the hash of the previous
   row and fresh randomness), committed with what the batch kept; it keeps the last 65,536. Both tables are
   added beside layout 4 where missing; nothing else reads them.
6. **Smaller per-read costs.** A walk's close records the kept file's digest only when a row changed. The held
   commitments before the selection are counted in SQLite; those after it are still placed by their
   directories, in order, before the selection's trail is verified.
7. **A loaded valid class takes its totals for the backing read.** A verdict row is shared by every backing its
   checkpoint scopes and written again by a walk for another backing, so totals now come from the namespace for
   the backing the caller reads. Found by the two-backing kept-walk test; a single walk loads its rows the same
   way, so the fix applies there too.
8. **Specification.** pool-v3 §14 gains "Kept positions": the three checks bind a kept class where a read uses
   it (state, ground or index), and a reader may keep and resume its classification positions under the stated
   conditions, with the verdict a read of every checkpoint gives. It changes no byte, identity or verdict, and
   corrects where the existing checks bind, as §14's own C0a text ("Kept classes replace reclassifying every
   held checkpoint on each read") requires; so it is recorded as a correction of adopted v3.

**C0a:** one mechanism, generalising kept classes from classes to the positions a walk reached; it replaces
re-judging every kept class on each read. No field, identity or authority is added. Costs: four small tables in
the kept file (layout 7: a kept file of layout 6 is replayed once), two in the evidence store, and one lineage
row per evidence batch (the last 65,536 kept).

**Alternatives:**
- *Status quo:* linear per read; fails the admission and steady-state budgets.
- *Only §14's three checks per class, no re-judging:* still linear per read (a lookup, a decode and a hash per
  class: seconds at tens of thousands of checkpoints).
- *A journal cache of its last read, extrapolating the clock:* a second implementation of C2b.6.1; each commit
  still a linear read; wallets unchanged.
- *A non-service count over a window of publications:* rejected in review, since a request's index is the first
  at which its identity was witnessed (C2b.5.2), so a windowed read counts a republication. Unchanged.

**Evidence:** The admission probe is flat after the change (74–178 ms from 5 to 320 held checkpoints, against 1.1 s at 80 before). Tests (`test/pool-v3-kept-state.test.ts`, "pool-v3 §14 kept walk"): judged counts
per read with a lower-index read on a temporary walk and a reopen; restored and other retained evidence begin
again, in-memory evidence leaves the kept walk; the silence clock through a gap, its boundary and a lapsed later
checkpoint; a term kept open and a successor link effective after the kept index; two backings' walks
alternating. Each read equals a fresh read (classes, canonical state, clock, force, ranges, carrying, faults).
`test/pool-v3-kept-walk.test.ts` (the code review's reproducers, asserting the correct behaviour): evidence restored

## 2026-10-03 — Keep the Ergo view in append-only SQLite rows and reopen it without re-verifying (slice 11 M11a)

**Status:** accepted; review and delivery are in WORK.md.

**Question:** the [direction](#2026-10-01--build-redemption-into-the-wallet-before-packaging-commands-and-give-the-design-point-and-release-assurance-slices-of-their-own)'s
decision 4 found the Ergo view's limits at the design point (10⁶ statements over three years, about 788,000
Ergo blocks). `ErgoVenue` kept every attributed section, header id and (durable) raw section in memory. Its
header store kept every accepted header as parsed objects. Each sync copied the whole best chain, and a range
from index zero walked every index. A reopen re-checked every header's work and every section's root, and
`ErgoVenueJournal` rewrote one JSON checkpoint of everything at each sync. A probe (synthetic chain at
difficulty 1, one commitment per 100 blocks) measured at 10⁴ blocks: 3.3 KB of heap per block (2.6 GB at three
years, against the 1 GiB budget) and 74 s to reopen (about 98 minutes at three years, against "restart
resumes without re-verifying retained history"). The checkpoint string passes V8's 512 MiB limit after
about ten weeks of mainnet sections. What storage keeps the view's verdicts and answers while memory and
per-sync work stop growing with history?

**Decision:**
1. **Rows, not a checkpoint.** `ErgoVenueJournal` keeps the view in STRICT SQLite tables, format
   `moe/ergo-view/2`:
   - `meta`: venue, owner fence, view rules, clock, pin, burying header, failure and retained bytes.
   - `headers`: every accepted header above the anchor, with its parent, its chain score as decimal text
     (scores pass 2⁶³ within months of mainnet work) and its bytes.
   - `best`: the best chain's id per height.
   - `side`: the headers off the best chain.
   - `charges` and `protected`: per supplier name.
   - `sections`: each index's header id and raw transaction views through the clock. These are venue-ergo
     §4's lossless evidence, retained per pool-v3 §13.2.
   - `objects`: indexed by (kind, subject, index) and by a key hashing kind, subject and record.

   A view without a journal opens the same tables in a private in-memory database, so there is one path.
   A file in the old `moe/ergo-checkpoint/1` format is refused; the view is synced again.
2. **The header store reads ancestry from rows.** `ergoHeaderStore` keeps only the anchor's context in
   memory and takes everything above it through `ErgoHeaderRows`; a Map-backed default serves standalone use.
   - A best-chain ancestor is found by height, and a side header's by walking the parent column.
   - A stored header's bytes are parsed only where a rule reads them, and their id, parent and height are
     then checked against the row.
   - Acceptance, score, first-accepted tie precedence (now the stored best chain itself), `forkHeight`,
     pruning and protection keep their rules.
   - The journal's rows hold a sync's changes in memory and write them in its commit.
3. **One commit per sync.** Sections read in a sync stage in memory, bounded by `sectionBytesPerSync`, whose
   default falls from 256 to 32 MiB; the header budgets already bound a sync to about 2,000 blocks a
   supplier. One transaction then appends:
   - headers, best-chain changes, side, charge and protection rows, and prunings;
   - sections and objects;
   - the clock.

   This happens before the snapshot (now the clock and its block) is exposed, with no await in between.
   A crash before the commit leaves the previous state, and an uncertain commit poisons the instance, as before.
4. **Indexed reads.** `range` streams the subject's objects between its indices in index and section order,
   ordered as §13 orders them (`orderedEntries`). An object's key requires its 32-byte subject, so keys
   are injective. It recomputes each row's key and refuses
   (`RangeLimitError`) as soon as the count or the record bytes pass the answer's budget, so no read holds
   more than an answer may. `witnessedAt`, and through it the publisher's `holds`, look up the key.
5. **Reopen checks what is cheap; `audit` checks everything.**
   - A reopen re-checks no work (venue-ergo §10: each header's work is checked once) and no root. It checks:
     - the format;
     - the view rules (`ERGO_VIEW_RULES`): a release changing header or attribution rules names new
       rules, and a view kept under others is refused until synced again;
     - the fence and the venue;
     - that the sections run from zero to the clock and end at the pin;
     - that the pin is on the stored best chain;
     - that the stored burying header is at least the depth above the pin and meets the best chain at or
       above it.
   - `audit({ work })` re-checks every row: header linkage, difficulty and score (work where asked), every
     section's root against the best chain's header at its index, every object attributed again under the
     current rules, objects beyond the sections, header rows on neither the best chain nor a side branch,
     a side header outscoring the best chain, and the retained bytes. It is linear. Run it on a view
     restored from a backup, copied from elsewhere or after a disk fault.
6. **Hostile side branches are bounded durably.**
   - Side-branch charges are kept per supplier name with the rows, so a new process grants no fresh quota.
     Supplier names must therefore be distinct and well-formed text.
   - A supplier past its quota is still read for headers that extend the best tip. Those cost no quota, and
     only the best chain's advance forgives a charge, so otherwise a quota every supplier had spent would
     stop the clock for good, across restarts.
   - Every header pass protects the last header it reached, whatever ended it: budget, failure or its tip.
     Each supplier name keeps at most four protected paths (a header on the best chain needs none): a new
     header replaces any it extends, and the oldest path goes past four. A fork one supplier left therefore
     still stands for another that serves it later, without its headers being read and charged again.
   - Side rows are bounded by the quotas.
7. **`retainedBytes` bounds stored objects, default 8 GiB.** Objects are no longer in memory. The design
   point's venue objects are a fraction of its 4.2 GB of Ergo sections; its 15.6 GB are trail records, not
   venue objects. Filling 8 GiB at the box minimum costs about 3,100 ERG, though the holder of a location's
   key recovers box values and so pays only fees.

**Rationale:**
- The view's memory had three parts that grew with history (parsed headers, attributed sections, raw
  sections), and its restart one cost that did (re-verification). Rows remove all four without changing a
  rule. They keep every header and section (venue-ergo §10, pool-v3 §13.2), so nothing retained is pruned (M4).
- A deep heavier fork is still judged, because its ancestry is read from the rows by height. The reader thus
  never refuses the heaviest chain it can see (venue-ergo §3), and a reorganization past the depth still
  fails the venue.
- This decision replaces the [2026-09-27 choice](2026-09.md#2026-09-27--persist-reproducing-venue-evidence-and-the-owning-journals-publication-outbox)
  to re-derive the whole view at reopen. That re-derivation is what the budget forbids at three years, so
  it moves into `audit`. The rules version covers the case it protected best, a release that changes
  attribution or header rules.
- Pool-v3 §14's digest and recomputation rule governs kept *replay* state resumed at a checkpoint. The view's
  rows are venue evidence and the answers computed from it under the reader's control (§13.2). Disk
  corruption, copied files and malicious rollback stay outside the local guarantee, as they were for the
  checkpoint's digest, and are what `audit` is for.
- Alternatives considered:
  - The direction's window of the difficulty lookback below the pin in memory, the rest evicted. It still
    needs rows for a fork below the window, so rows alone are simpler.
  - Keep the in-memory graph and compact it into typed arrays. That is still linear in history, about
    70 MB a year.
  - Keep re-verification at reopen. That costs 98 minutes per restart at three years.
  - A rolling digest per commit. It detects no corruption of older rows without a linear read, which
    `audit` already is.
  - Commit per chunk of sections inside one sync. That is equally consistent, but the 32 MiB staging bound
    makes it unnecessary.
- Protecting every pass, rather than only those stopped by a budget, closes a gap the one path exposed. A
  deep heavier fork whose supplier failed mid-pass used to be pruned each sync, so it never failed the venue.
  A view without a journal used to keep everything, so it never hit that gap.

**Evidence:**

## 2026-10-03 — Hold the note tree's last leaf and judge §7's position bound first (audit, area 27)

**Status:** accepted (rolling audit of the v3 state machine: `state.ts`, `recovery.ts`, the replay
store's state reads and append, `spent-set.ts`, `capsules.ts`, `verify-ahead.ts` and the reader's
trail replay; no specification change). The journal (`store.ts`), served trail and readers
(area 31) stay for later audits.

**Question:** does one record's judgment and apply match pool-v3 §§5–7 and pool-recovery
C3.1–C3.8 in every mode (admission, replay, adoption, force), leave the state unchanged on every
refusal, and use one mechanism per rule? The audit found two boundary defects. pool-v2 §4 (adopted
by pool-v3 §2) says the note tree holds 2^32 leaves, but the replay store's append folded the last
leaf into a 33rd frontier slot that its 32-slot encoding could not hold. The record a reader must
accept therefore ended in a RangeError: an unresolved read, not a verdict. §7's position bound was
checked after the evidence hash had already framed the position as a u64, so it could not fire.
`StateHandle.at` read a position past the tip as genesis, contrary to C2b.3.1.

**Decision:**
1. The frontier keeps the whole tree in slot 32 once it holds 2^32 leaves. Rows written before
   read that slot as absent. §14's snapshot check takes the root from it exactly at a full tree

2. The position bound (CAPACITY) is judged first, before any state read or framing.
3. `at()` refuses a position past the tip.
4. A settlement's door reads only the acceptance deadline, which C3.4 already bounds by the

**Rationale:** each fix is the smallest change that makes the specified bound a verdict. The other
option for item 1 was refusing the 2^32-th leaf, which would contradict pool-v2 §4. A 33rd slot
costs 33 bytes per namespace row and needs no schema version, since old rows still read. Other
simplifications the review raised stay as they are:
- The single-backing and scoped forms of `SegmentReplay` (verdict-equal; unify when the journal is
  next touched).
- Zero padding tags stored as demand-tag rows (they would change stored facts and reports for no
  verdict).
- The no-index replay mode, in which `locked()` and revocation do not apply. Only the harness's
  no-venue replay uses it, and that replay retires or stays under WORK.md Next 7 (M10e2).

**Evidence:** one fresh adversarial review of the area found no blocker or major issue. Its

## 2026-10-02 — Run the wallet and relay commands over the library, with kept evidence, acceptance files and a status read (slice 10 M10c2b1)

**Status:** accepted after one fresh review (below).

**Question:** the [M10b decision](#2026-10-02--install-one-moe-command-over-role-directories-on-ergo-venues-only-with-keys-in-files-and-funding-apart-from-the-wallet-slice-10-m10b)
fixed the wallet and relay commands' surface, files and custody. Building them over the wallet library left
choices it did not make: how a holder's acceptance reaches `settle`, which evidence a read takes when the service
does not answer, how a caller reads a saved record's resolution, how `wallet publish` knows the gap is open, how a
relay reports what it sent, and how a backer restores K beside its seed.

**Decision:**
1. *Acceptance files.* `accept --out f` writes the acceptance as its canonical kind-2 publication bytes

2. *Kept evidence.* Each successful sync keeps the package it was served in `packages/<backing>`; where the

   read still judges the frontier at the view's witnessed index, so a stale package cannot pass for a newer one.
3. *`status <alias>`.* Reads the saved payment or act as the last `sync` resolved it, with no read or proof; a
   saved demand prints its absolute deadline, so a rerun after a lost reply names it exactly (a `+n` deadline is
   relative to each read).
4. *`WalletView.gap`.* `sync` reports whether the read's horizon is past the canonical checkpoint by more than the
   backing's silence duration, the test `route` already applied (`gapOpen`); `wallet publish` refuses `GAP` unless
   its read shows it.
5. *Publishing.* `ErgoVenue.publish` answers the publication the publisher built last for the record (an earlier one
   it replaced may land instead; `publishRecord` delegates and stays `void`), and `witnessedAt` the first index the
   view witnessed an exact record at. `relay publish` prints that transaction id, and a rerun once witnessed prints
   the index (`--wait n` syncs until then, bounded in indices and polls). Its publisher's
   outbox is one revisioned snapshot in `relay.db` under the publisher's load, save and guard contract, fenced as the
   journal's is.
6. *Smaller choices.* `pay` and `freshen` submit after preparing (a refused or unanswered submission leaves the
   record saved for the rerun); `restore-seed` and `restore` take `--backer-key f` to copy K into the new directory;

   what libraries log (bb.js reports each proof on `console.log`) to stderr, keeping stdout to the one JSON object.

**Rationale:** each choice reuses an existing encoding or library path: the publication bytes are already the
acceptance's public form, the kept package is what the slice 9 drill already handed a gap reader, and `gap` is

## 2026-10-02 — Read a note as presented from saved demands and the record's tags, save which demands a re-demand repeats, and freshen into one note (slice 10 M10c2a)

**Status:** accepted after one fresh review, its material findings resolved.

**Question:** the [M10b decision](#2026-10-02--install-one-moe-command-over-role-directories-on-ergo-venues-only-with-keys-in-files-and-funding-apart-from-the-wallet-slice-10-m10b)
(item 9) fixed which notes are presented and what `prepare`, `burn`,
`demand` and `freshen` may select. Building it left choices it did not make:
how a wallet reads an ended or forced demand's tags, where a demand's link to
earlier demands is kept so a retry prints it, what `freshen` creates and which
of a demand's notes it takes, and how a caller learns which demand to freshen.

**Decision:**
1. *Presented, from two sources.* A holding's `presented` lists the demands of
   this seed naming it: saved demands whose inputs name it (any status), and
   demands of its backing the read holds naming its tag, ended or not
   (`presentedWithTag`: the replay store's tag query without the end filter, and
   `ForceState.forced`, every forced demand, which an end does not remove;
   `added` keeps its meaning for every existing reader). A record demand counts
   only where its presenter key is this seed's derivation.
2. *Selection.* `prepare` and `burn` take available unpresented notes only and
   refuse `FUNDS` naming `freshen`. `demand` tries an exact selection of
   unpresented notes, then, for each earlier demand in identity order, an exact
   selection among the available notes it presents; otherwise `FUNDS`.
3. *Links kept.* The demands presenting any selected note are saved with the
   demand (`saved_records.repeats`, kind 4 only) and returned as `Act.repeats`,
   so an exact retry and `act` report what the original call did, a
   seed-restored wallet's record-found links included. Wallet profile
   `moe/wallet/v3/8`; earlier databases and backups are refused, as at every
   profile change before release.
4. *Freshen.* `freshen(alias, demand)` spends the demand's available presented
   notes (one with a fresh zero input, or its pair) into one fresh note of
   their sum and three fresh zero outputs, shuffled: a kind-2 payment saved,
   submitted, reproved and resolved through the payment paths, with intent
   `[backing, "freshen", demand]` and `Payment.freshens` naming the demand
   (`payee` is the fresh note). It refuses `ABSENT` where no unspent note of
   this seed names the demand, `LOCKED` while the demand stands, `CONFLICT`
   where saved records reserve the notes, and, as any payment, `SILENCE` or
   `CONFLICT` where admission is closed. One fresh note of the sum serves a
   later demand of the same quantity.
5. *Checked again at save.* A demand saved while another call proved may have
   presented that call's note, and once that demand is final or failed no
   reservation holds it; the operator does not judge presentedness. Inside the
   save transaction a payment or burn refuses `CONFLICT` for a note any saved
   demand presents, and a demand for one presented by a demand outside its
   `repeats`. A freshen is exempt: another demand presenting its note shares a
   tag already linked. A restored backup's demand links and freshen intents are
   checked for shape at install.

**Rationale:** reading presented-ness from public evidence as well as saved
state keeps a seed-restored wallet from reusing a note whose tag the record
already shows; the saved half covers demands refused at the door or published
without force, which the record does not show. Keeping the links in a column
costs a profile change before any release, against recomputing them at each
retry from a view the retry does not read. Freshen as a payment reuses
reservation, reproof and resolution unchanged rather than adding an act kind.

**Evidence:**
- *Tests:* `test/pool-v3-redemption.test.ts` "pays and burns no presented
  note, presents again one earlier demand's notes only, and freshens them";
  the gap withdrawal test reads presented notes from a forced, withdrawn
  demand in a seed-restored copy; two C3.8 void tests spend the voiding note
  by `freshen`. Unit suite, `check:scripts`, `check:pool:v3-redemption`
  (real proofs; no freshen step) at e50e537; unit suite again after the fixes.
- *Review:* one fresh adversarial review with reproducers. Taken: the save
  step re-checked reservation only, so a payment proved across another
  demand's stand-and-withdraw spent a presented note, and a demand mixed a
  presented and an unpresented one with empty `repeats` (item 5, regression
  test with a held prover); no test of two unlinked earlier demands (added)
  and a test comment claiming a refusal that the rule allows; `FUNDS` naming
  `freshen` in a gap, where it refuses `SILENCE`; restored rows with malformed
  links or freshen intents failing later rather than at install. Found sound:
  selection order and linking, freshen's scope and refusals, reproof of a

  existing readers, the profile change and backup round trip.
- *Limits:* a seed-restored wallet misses demands refused at the door,

  freshen runs in M10c2b's command drill.

fixed the command surface; building the `reader` and `operator` commands left
choices it did not make: where the anchor's context and the proving parameters
live, how `venue create` knows which reference context it is creating, what a
reader keeps between processes, how `serve` keeps its own journal calls from
meeting the service's, how it learns that statements were admitted since its
last checkpoint, how the funding key's spend budget is kept, and how long
`return` and `adopt` wait.

**Decision:**
1. *Files.* Each directory keeps the anchor and the 1,024 headers below it in
   `anchor.json`, fetched once (at `venue create` or `init --venue`) and
   authenticated again by linkage to the anchor id whenever a view opens (one
   an interrupted `venue create` kept for another anchor is fetched again and
   replaced); the view itself is `venue.db`. The parameters' fetch and check is the package's
   (`src/pool/parameter-files.ts`); `init` fetches them or, with
   `--parameters`, copies a local copy, each kept only when its hash is the
   pinned one. `config.json` is written last, so a directory without it is an
   interrupted `init` (`INCOMPLETE`); a rerun refuses (`EXISTS`).
2. *Venue creation asks the creator's own node which network it is.* A
   difficulty-1 synthetic chain is also below the testnet context's anchor
   bound, so headers alone do not tell a synthetic node from a testnet one at
   creation. `venue create` therefore refuses unless `/info` names `testnet`
   (or `synthetic` with `--synthetic`). Readers still judge only headers; this
   keeps a creator from naming a context it did not mean.
3. *A reader keeps evidence, not replay state.* `supply` and `presentation`
   sync the view, bring the service's evidence into `evidence.db` (§14
   incremental transport) or read `--package`, and replay from it in memory, so
   every proof is verified again in each process. The output carries the
   sync's report (unresolved index, stopped suppliers): the read is final at
   its judging index, and a view that could not read further says so. Kept replay state across
   processes waits for slice 11's measurement; the wallet keeps its own.
4. *Serve queues every journal call.* The view's sync, serve's schedule and
   the service's journal calls (through a proxy of the journal) run one at a
   time, so no wallet call meets `BUSY` from serve's own work, or `STALE`
   because the view moved during it. A slow node answer inside serve's tick
   delays service calls until the node client's timeout.
5. *`V3OperatorJournal.status()`* (signs and publishes nothing and changes no
   signed state or revision; like every command it records what it read of
   the venue under the owner fence) reports the clock, the latest signed commitment (signing index, recorded
   publication, held exactly), the statements admitted past it, the index of
   the latest held commitment and a pending return. Serve's rule (M10b item 7)
   reads it: publish while not held; with it held and no return pending,
   commit `serve:<index>` when statements were admitted and `--interval`
   indices passed since it was signed, or at half the least silence duration
   of the served terms after the venue witnessed it. The latest signed commitment, a
   pending return's opening included, is published on every poll until the
   venue holds it. The witnessed index only rises, and a commit needs the
   previous commitment held, so a command id is never reused.
6. *Spend budget.* Before each broadcast the signed transaction replays on an
   offline `MempoolNode` funded with the key's unspent plain boxes; what it
   takes from the key is reserved in `spend.db` before the transaction is sent,
   so a refused or uncertain broadcast keeps its reservation, and an exact
   resubmission passes. Past the budget it refuses (`BUDGET`). The publisher
   reads any supplier failure as "not taken", which the journal reports as
   `UNAVAILABLE`, so the budget keeps its last refusal and the command reports
   `BUDGET` in place of that `UNAVAILABLE` (only); `serve` logs it and keeps
   polling. A transaction that does not replay over the boxes the node lists
   (an index behind its mempool) is `UNREPLAYED`, reserves nothing and is tried
   again.
7. *Waiting.* `return` retries `SCHEDULE`, `adopt` retries `SCHEDULE` and
   `UNAVAILABLE` (the return opening not yet witnessed), syncing between tries
   for at most four lags of witnessed indices; a stalled chain leaves them
   waiting until interrupted, since no wall-clock bound is a protocol time.
8. *Dependencies.* `@aztec/bb.js` and `@noir-lang/noir_js` are exact-pinned
   dependencies (M10b item 11); role modules load on demand, and neither
   reader nor operator command imports the prover. No backend logger is
   attached, so a command's stdout carries only its answer (the drill checks
   it).

**Rationale:** keeping the context beside the view costs about 450 KB per
directory and saves a 1,025-header request on every command, which a node
would also see. A proxy over the journal reuses the service unchanged rather
than adding a lock to `service-http.ts` that only `serve` needs. A reader that
keeps no replay state is slower but has no kept-state invalidation to get
wrong across processes before slice 11 measures whether it matters. The budget
reuses the live drills' offline replay, which already judges exactly what a
publication costs.

**Evidence:**
- *Drill:* `scripts/pool/v3/command-drill.mjs` (in `check:pool:v3`) runs the
  reader and operator commands in fresh processes on separate directories over
  the synthetic node through `ergoNodeSupplier` and `ergoNodePublisher`:
  operator init, funding, venue create (refused without `--synthetic`, rerun
  prints the same file; over a stale kept context it anchors again), open under
  fixture-signed terms (refused without `--synthetic` and under another name;
  exact rerun; past a 1,000 nanoErg budget `BUDGET`), reader init, terms
  add/show (refused without `--synthetic` and with a forged signature), serve
  with a keep-alive checkpoint at half silence, a second command refused
  `BUSY`, `supply` over the service and, with it stopped, over a package file
  on one verifier instance (the same answer), `presentation` of an unknown
  demand (`ABSENT`), then past silence `return`, `adopt` (no receipts) and serve
  again; a stopped service reads as `UNAVAILABLE`; group-readable, other-role,
  interrupted and existing directories refused; SQLite's temporary files land
  in the directory through `openDirectory` (Linux, `/proc/self/fd`), where they
  land in `/var/tmp`
  without it. `check:package` runs the installed bin.
- *Review:* one fresh adversarial review with reproducers. Taken: a budget
  refusal surfaced as `UNAVAILABLE` (item 6); an interrupted `venue create`
  left a context for another anchor that no command repaired (item 1); the
  drill's evidence claim was wider than its steps (presentation, package file
  and `--verifiers` added; the rest stated below); refusals that exited 3
  (parameters not found at `init` or missing at a later read, a missing parent
  directory, a port in use) now exit 1, and `init` without `--node` is a usage error before anything is created;
  `status()`'s description (item 5); serve now publishes a pending return's
  opening; a stalled sync is reported. Kept as stated: `EncodingError` from
  the library's input checks exits 1 (`INVALID`) as a refusal of the input; a
  testnet-context venue file names no difficulty floor, so a reader whose own
  nodes serve a difficulty-1 chain under a venue file relabelled as testnet
  reads it without the synthetic note (the testnet anchor bound only keeps
  mainnet out; the live drill, M10d, revisits it). It found sound: the lock and
  setup order, file modes, secrets never printed, the service file's content,
  the client's expected identity, term and venue authentication, serve's
  single commitment in flight and command ids across a killed restart, the
  queue's freedom from deadlock, the budget's durable reservation, `admitted`
  after a return, and no `@noir-lang` import reachable from the commands.
- *Limits:* no statements are admitted in this drill, so serve's
  commit-on-admission rule and each command's peak RSS (M10b item 10) wait for
  M10c2's wallet commands; a verify-only process's loaded modules are checked
  in M10c2's packed-install drill; the online parameter fetch and two or more
  endpoints are exercised only by unit tests and reading; Windows modes and ACLs unchecked; per-process
  side-header quotas and view reopen cost (M10b item 5).

**Spec change:** none needed.

## 2026-10-02 — Serve the synthetic chain through the node REST paths, answering as recorded real nodes do (slice 10 M10c1)

**Status:** accepted after one fresh review (test tooling for slice 10's
drill; no runtime or specification change).

**Question:** the [M10b decision](#2026-10-02--install-one-moe-command-over-role-directories-on-ergo-venues-only-with-keys-in-files-and-funding-apart-from-the-wallet-slice-10-m10b)
(item 5) runs every command's process through `ergoNodeSupplier` and
`ergoNodePublisher` against a test-only synthetic node, whose mempool and index
answers must match a real node's, since the publisher tells a supplier that
lacks a transaction from one that does not answer by them. What does a node
answer, and does `MempoolNode`, the mempool the synthetic venue already uses,
answer the same?

**Decision:**
1. Real answers to the publisher's six calls are recorded from a public
   explorer-enabled node, with a second node required to give the same
   statuses (`experiments/ergo-range/record-node-answers.mjs`,
   `test/fixtures/ergo-node-answers.json`).
2. `MempoolNode` answers a box lookup as `/utxo/withPool/byIdBinary` does:
   from blocks and from every output of a pooled transaction, even one the
   pool spends. Its unspent listing still leaves out pool-spent boxes, as the
   index's `excludeMempoolSpent` does. `reset` (a node that lost its mempool)
   and `spend` (a box spent in a block) replace tests' edits of its box map.
3. `scripts/pool/v3/synthetic-node.mjs`, not shipped, serves `Chain` and
   `MempoolNode` on loopback through the read paths, the six publisher paths
   (404 with the node's error body for a missing transaction or box; a
   pooled or mined transaction refused again; the mempool's boxes after every
   index page) and two drill paths, fund and mine. Statements carry every
   field the clients read; boxes carry no `address`.
4. `synthetic-node-check.mjs` (in `check:scripts`) compares each recorded
   answer with its synthetic counterpart and what `ergoNodePublisher` makes of
   both, then reads back the four record kinds a view published through it.

**Rationale:** aligning `MempoolNode` keeps one mempool model for the library
tests and the drill; a wrapper answering differently from the tests' mempool
would let the two disagree on the same publisher path. The publisher's verdicts
do not change: a refused transaction's input that another of its own pooled
transactions spends is now judged live, so the publisher keeps the transaction
rather than rebuilding, the conservative outcome. Recording a mainnet node is
enough because the answers' shapes are the node software's, not the chain's.

**Evidence:**
- *Recorded:* nodes 6.1.5 (recorded) and 6.0.2 (same statuses), 16 answers. A
  probe of pooled transactions' first inputs found withPool answering 200 for
  15 of 17 and 18 of 21 while each stayed pooled; the rest answered 404 on
  both withPool and the plain UTXO path (stale pool entries), so the recorder
  takes an entry whose input the UTXO set holds. An unknown pay-to-public-key
  tree lists `[]`; a malformed point answers 500.
- *Observed, not changed:* the index appends the mempool's boxes to every page,
  so for a key with 100 or more confirmed boxes the publisher's 100-box page
  cut leaves them out; its own unsettled change is held by the publisher, so
  only a stranger's unmined payment to the key goes unused.
- *Review:* one fresh adversarial review found no blocker and nothing
  material; it confirmed against Ergo's source that `withPool` answers any
  pooled output and that every rewritten publisher test takes its former path.
  Taken: a pooled or mined transaction submitted again was accepted (and its
  stored proofs replaced, stalling readers), now refused as a node does; the
  node's tree reader now matches `MempoolNode`'s; the paging rule is recorded
  and compared; the recorder avoids stale pool entries and asserts the second
  node's agreement; a created-then-spent pool box is tested. Left open: the
  synthetic node's index never lags its blocks, so the publisher's
  "index has not read its blocks" refusal is covered only by unit answers (a
  lag knob for the drill, WORK.md); `chainSlice` differs from a node for
  `fromHeight = toHeight` and a missing `toHeight`, which no client asks.

**Spec change:** none needed.

## 2026-10-02 — Install one `moe` command over role directories, on Ergo venues only, with keys in files and funding apart from the wallet (slice 10 M10b)

**Status:** accepted after one fresh review and its read-back (slice 10's
command surface, decided before M10c's code; no specification change).

**Question:** slice 10 ships the holder wallet (with the backer role), the
operator service and the supply reader as commands from a packed install.
WORK.md's Next 3 left M10b to decide the commands, their data directories,
key custody and backup, configuration, venue clients, verifier sizing and
Next 2's privacy duties, and whether a command accepts only verifiers
`openV3Verifier` built (the [M10a](#2026-10-02--ship-the-six-compiled-relations-in-the-package-and-require-every-readers-verifier-to-name-them-slice-10-m10a)
limit). Today every party is wired by a harness: `drill.mjs` compiles the
relations, `redemption-store-check.mjs` runs each wallet operation in a fresh
process over a venue it passes in, keys are fixture constants, and the CRS
comes from `scripts/pool/prepare-crs.mjs`. The fixture and synthetic venues
live in one process's memory, and the pilot CLI runs the frozen transparent
path.

**Decision:**
1. *One executable, `moe`, with a role per data directory.* `package.json`
   gains `bin: { "moe": "dist/cli/moe.js" }`. `moe <role> init --dir <D>`
   creates a new directory and fixes its role (`wallet`, `operator`,
   `reader`, `relay`); every later command names `--dir` and refuses a
   directory of another role. Each invocation is one process that does one
   operation and exits, except `moe operator serve`. Every command first
   takes an exclusive SQLite lock on `D/lock.db`, held for the process's
   life and released by the operating system if it dies, and refuses `BUSY`
   before opening anything else, so two commands never fence each other in
   one directory.
2. *Reruns.* Every mutating wallet and journal command requires the alias
   or command id the library keys on (never defaulted), so a rerun after a
   crash or a lost reply is the library's exact retry and prints the saved
   result. Exceptions, stated per command:
   - `fulfill` is never replayed (the library refuses a second call even if
     identical): a rerun exits 4 (`REPLAY`) printing the saved fulfillment
     marked as a replay, and `fulfillment <alias>` reads it. A caller credits
     only on exit 0.
   - `init` and `venue create` create exclusively; a rerun of `venue create`
     prints the existing file, a rerun of `init` refuses.
   - `restore` is confirmed by `custody().restoredFrom` equal to the digest
     at the destination; `restore-seed` refuses an existing destination.
   - `handoff` (item 6) reuses its key file.
   - The operator's `publish` targets the latest outbox and `adopt` is keyed
     by the opening sequence, as the journal defines them.
   - `relay publish` is keyed by the record (item 8).
3. *Commands, as thin wrappers over existing methods.*
   - `wallet`: `init`, `seed --show`, `restore-seed`, `handoff`, `restore`,
     `terms add|show`, `service add`, `request`, `pay` (`prepare` then
     `submit`), `reprove`, `submit`, `sync`, `fulfill`, `fulfillment`,
     `demand`, `withdraw`, `settle`, `freshen` (item 9), `presentation`,
     `publish` and `publish-acceptance` (item 8). With `--backer` at `init`
     the directory also holds K, and `venue create`, `terms create`, `issue`,
     `accept` and `burn` are enabled.
   - `operator`: `init`, `venue create`, `open` (genesis under signed terms),
     `serve`, `return`, `adopt`. `serve` owns the journal (item 7); `open`,
     `return` and `adopt` run while it is stopped, which the directory lock
     enforces. `open` and `return` publish what they sign before exiting.
     A journal reopened over signed state may not sign until the venue
     reaches its reopening index plus the lag (C2.8.2), so `return` and
     `adopt` sync their venue view in-process until then before acting
     (`adopt` also until the return opening is witnessed), and every restart
     of `serve` costs that lag, about 11 blocks at depth 10, before it
     admits or commits. No `moe` command reaches the service's admin `commit`
     and `publish` (the directory lock would refuse it while `serve` runs);
     they stay for library callers, and no new wire command is added.
     Takeover and rescope stay library operations until a drill needs them.
   - `reader`: `init`, `terms add|show`, `service add`, `supply` (the
     frontier read: issued, burned, position, canonical checkpoint and
     force) and `presentation`.
   - `relay`: `init` (an Ergo funding key) and `publish <file>`.
   - Evidence comes from a service or from `--package <file>` (any whole
     package, as the library's `supply` takes any transport). In a gap a
     wallet reads its own kept evidence from its last sync, as the slice 9
     drill does; a replica role that serves evidence while the operator is
     offline is the durable-availability gate's, not slice 10's.
4. *Data directories and files.* Before any file opens, a command sets
   `process.umask(0o077)` and `SQLITE_TMPDIR` to the directory, so the
   files SQLite and the libraries create (databases, WAL and journal files,
   evidence, replay and digest files, temporary files) are owner-only and
   stay inside it. `init` creates the directory `0700` and refuses an
   existing one; any command refuses a directory that group or others can
   read. Public inputs are JSON (`venue.json`, `config.json`, the files under
   `services/`), bytes as lowercase hex and integers as decimal strings;
   nothing signed is JSON. Signed root terms are kept as their canonical
   bytes and signature in `terms/<name>` and `terms/<name>.sig`, so no new
   framing is added. A wallet directory binds one venue (the wallet database
   binds it) and holds a service file per operator,
   `services/<operator>.json`. Each party keeps its own Ergo view in
   `venue.db` (`ErgoVenueJournal`). On Windows, the host platform, modes are
   not enforced and nothing checks ACLs: a stated limit for release
   assurance and device custody.
5. *Venues: Ergo only, synthetic for drills, testnet live.*
   - `venue.json` holds only the identity preimage (reference context,
     anchor id, depth, the four location trees) and the anchor height. A
     command derives the venue identity from it and requires it to be the
     identity the terms name; the guard keeps refusing mainnet. Node
     endpoints are each directory's own, in `config.json` (two or more
     recommended), so the venue's creator does not become every party's
     default node and see each party's sync timing.
   - `venue create` runs only in a backer wallet or operator directory: it
     takes the directory's endpoints, anchors at `fullHeight − depth`,
     verifies the anchor through `ErgoVenue` (with the testnet difficulty
     bound of M9c2), and derives four distinct location trees from a fresh
     `location.key` (one P2PK key per kind, derived from the key by kind),
     so the creator can collect the boxes' value (venue-ergo §1).
   - The synthetic context is accepted only when `terms add` and `venue
     create` are given `--synthetic`, and `terms show` says its headers
     carry no real work.
   - Every Ergo party reads through `ergoNodeSupplier` and publishes through
     `ergoNodePublisher`, so the synthetic drill exercises the client the
     live one uses: a test-only synthetic node
     (`scripts/pool/v3/synthetic-node.mjs`, not shipped) serves a `Chain`
     and a `MempoolNode` through the node REST paths those clients call,
     plus a mining endpoint. Its mempool and index answers are tested
     against recorded real node responses, since the publisher's "lacks"
     rule depends on them. The fixture venue is not offered to commands; it
     stays the library tests' venue. Slice 10's acceptance changes
     accordingly: "on the local and synthetic Ergo venues" becomes "on the
     synthetic Ergo node".
   - Limits: each process resets the per-supplier side-header quota, and
     each command reopens `venue.db`, re-verifying its retained headers,
     until slice 11's rebuild of the view.
6. *Keys, custody and backup.* No secret is read from argv or the
   environment or written to stdout or stderr, except the seed under
   `wallet seed --show`; secrets come from files or stdin.
   - The wallet seed stays inside `wallet.db`. `restore-seed` reads it from
     stdin.
   - `handoff` (the library's freezing export, named for what it does)
     writes its key file exclusively and syncs it before calling
     `exportBackup`, reuses an existing key file on rerun, writes the
     encrypted bytes to a second file and prints the digest. Both paths must
     lie outside the data directory and differ. `restore` takes the three.
   - K (Ed25519) is `backer.key`, read only by `terms create`, `issue` and
     `accept` and handed to the wallet as its `BackerSigner`, so it never
     enters the wallet database or a handoff. The backer backs it up as a
     file to offline media. Losing K loses issuance and acceptance, so every
     later demand reads as dishonour; `terms show` says so. Holding K on an
     offline machine needs `issue` and `accept` split around the signer in
     the library, which slice 10 does not do.
   - The operator key is `operator.key` and the admin credential
     `admin.token`, both owner-only and never handed out. The service file a
     holder receives holds only the URL and the service-wide wallet token,
     never per holder ([visibility](../docs/POOL_V3_VISIBILITY.md)). The
     client's expected operator and venue preimage come from the signed
     terms and `venue.json`, never from the service file. An operator
     directory is never restored from a copy: a copy older than the live
     journal would sign again at sequences already signed, which fencing
     cannot see. Its recovery is succession.
   - Ergo funding keys (secp256k1, `funding.key`) live only in operator and
     relay directories; losing one loses only the coins it holds. Each
     funding directory's `config.json` carries a spend budget in nanoErg,
     and its publisher refuses a transaction past it, as the live drills'
     guard does. The operator's publisher keeps its pending transactions in
     the journal (`publisherPersistence`). A wallet directory holds no
     funding key.
7. *How `serve` checkpoints.* `serve` runs the loopback service, syncs its
   venue view on a poll, and commits and publishes on the witnessed index,
   not on arrivals: it commits when statements are admitted since the last
   checkpoint and the interval it is given has passed, and at the latest at
   half the backing's silence duration after the canonical checkpoint, with
   at most one commitment in flight and command id `serve:<index>` (the
   witnessed index at the decision). At start and on every poll it publishes
   the latest signed commitment while the venue does not hold it
   (`publish()` targets the latest outbox and is safe to repeat), so a crash
   between commit and publish leaves nothing the journal refuses as stale
   after restart. Committing on a schedule rather than per statement avoids
   the thin intervals the visibility table's small-pool note warns of.
8. *Publications by relay.* A wallet's `publish` and `publish-acceptance`
   write a file holding the venue identity, the backing and the exact
   publication (kind, subject, record) the library would publish. `wallet
   publish` refuses an act whose read does not show the gap open, since
   outside it a publication has no force, a release discloses its output
   and a demand makes its tags public. `moe relay publish <file>` refuses a
   venue identity other than its own `venue.json`'s, decodes the record and
   requires its subject to be the backing, refuses a demand whose instant
   its view has not reached, then publishes with its funding key, keeping
   its publisher's pending transactions in `relay.db` behind the journal's
   load, save and fence contract. It prints the node's acceptance and the
   transaction id; a rerun prints the index that witnessed it once one has.
   This supplies the mechanism, and the duty stays open: a relay directory
   of the holder's own links all its gap acts to each other and to its
   funding, and a third party's relay needs a transport slice 10 does not
   have and learns the holder's channel. The visibility table gains the
   relay as an observer.
9. *Presented notes and `freshen`* (Next 2, §C1.5, C3.1).
   - A note is presented when its nullifier is an input of a saved demand
     of this wallet, whatever the demand's status (`saved_inputs`, no schema
     change), or when its tag is named by a demand of this seed found in the
     record, ended or not, including demands read with force (a tag query
     without the end filter, and the read's force list). A seed-restored
     wallet misses demands that were refused at the door or published
     without force; the wallet guide says so.
   - `prepare` and `burn` select no presented note. `freshen(alias, demand)`
     spends exactly one demand's presented notes (its one note with a zero
     pad, or its pair) into this seed's own fresh notes, never mixed with
     another demand's or unpresented notes, so it links no two demands; its
     intent binds the backing and the demand.
   - `demand` prefers an exact selection of unpresented notes. It may present
     again notes of one earlier demand of this seed (that demand's notes or a
     subset of them), never notes of two earlier demands together nor a
     presented note beside an unpresented one; otherwise it refuses `FUNDS`
     and points to `freshen` where that is admissible. Such a demand links
     only to the one earlier demand whose tags it repeats, and it keeps gap
     redemption open where `freshen`, a payment, is refused (`SILENCE`, an
     ended term). The output says which earlier demand it links to.
   - Existing tests that pay or burn from freed notes move to `freshen`
     first; those that re-demand freed notes stay.
10. *Verifiers.* A command builds its own verifier from the shipped
    relations (`openV3Verifier`, or the prover's, which is built the same
    way) and takes none from outside, so commands accept only verifiers
    `openV3Verifier` built; the library keeps `DeclaredVerifier` for tests,
    and a command's kept files live only in its own directory. Every wallet
    command builds a real verifier, those that read nothing included, since
    the wallet's constructor requires one; M10c measures that cost.
    Verify-only commands destroy the key-deriving backend instance once the
    verifier is built (M5b.6) and load the prover by dynamic import, and a
    packed-install check shows a reader process loads no `@noir-lang`
    module. `--verifiers n` sets the instance count, default 2, refused
    above 6. The measured 10⁵ first sync peaked at 543 MB with two instances
    and about 85 MB each, but without venue ranges, an Ergo view or a
    prover; six leaves about 140 MB for those, and proving commands' memory
    is unmeasured. M10c's drill records each command's peak RSS; slice 11
    measures against the budgets.
11. *Parameters and dependencies.* `prepare-crs.mjs`'s fetch-and-check moves
    into the package; `init` caches the two parameter files in the directory
    (2 MiB), each checked by its hash, and `--parameters <dir>` copies them
    from a local directory for an offline install. `@aztec/bb.js` and
    `@noir-lang/noir_js` become exact-pinned dependencies, so one install
    serves every role; a verify-only process still loads no `@noir-lang`
    module. bb.js's log goes to stderr through its logger option.
12. *Output and exit codes.* A command prints one JSON object on stdout
    (bytes hex, integers decimal strings), with a status field telling
    pending, final, failed and unavailable evidence apart. A refusal
    (`VenueError`, the wallet's and journal's coded errors,
    `V3ServiceClientError`, `ReferenceVenueError`, `ProgramError`,
    `ParameterError`, an unresolved read) prints `{ code, check, message }`
    on stderr and exits 1; a usage error exits 2; anything else exits 3 with
    `error.stack` alone, as unexpected failures stay visible; a replayed
    fulfillment exits 4. No output carries a secret or a note opening,
    except `seed --show` and `request`'s frame and digest, which are meant
    to be handed on. Deadlines and instants are witnessed indices, absolute
    or relative to the read (`+n`), never wall-clock time.
13. *Explanations* (Next 2, release target's wallet behavior). `terms add`
    authenticates the backing against a name the holder supplies (as a
    request's digest is), refuses on mismatch, and shows the obligor,
    payout, operator, venue, silence clock, what losing K means and the

## 2026-10-02 — Ship the six compiled relations in the package and require every reader's verifier to name them (slice 10 M10a)

**Status:** accepted (slice 10's first milestone; no specification change).

**Question:** an installed command must prove and verify under §11.4's
configuration without compiling anything, but `openV3Prover` took the six
compiled artifacts from its caller, and every harness compiled the sources
first. The [adoption entry](#2026-10-01--adopt-pool-v3-with-one-configuration-and-hold-its-manifest-in-the-runtime-m8a)
left shipped artifacts to the commands slice. Separately, readers, the
journal and the wallet still accepted a verifier naming no circuits (a test
double), which the [binding entry](2026-09.md#2026-09-30--bound-evidence-storage-by-its-rows-and-bind-reader-verifiers-to-the-configuration)
deferred until the commands hold only real verifiers. Such a verifier was
named per object in replay identities, so the wallet and journal kept their
reads only in memory.

**Decision:**
1. *Ship the relations as package data.* `src/pool/v3/programs.json` holds
   each relation's compiler version, ABI and bytecode (90 KB), copied into
   `dist/` by the build. The compiler's debug symbols and file map are left
   out: they hold the compiling machine's paths, and witness generation
   reads them only to enrich an execution failure's message.
2. *A loader checked against the manifest.* `adoptedPrograms()`
   (`programs.ts`) reads the file afresh at each call and refuses
   (`ProgramError`) unless it holds exactly the six relations, each with
   exactly those three fields, the manifest's compiler version, and
   canonical base64 bytecode whose identity is the manifest's.
   `openV3Verifier()` (`verifier.ts`) derives the keys from it and refuses
   unless every key identity is the configuration's. `openV3Prover(api)`
   takes no artifacts. Neither module that loads needs `@noir-lang/noir_js`,
   so a verify-only party installs only `@aztec/bb.js`.
3. *The ABI is bound by reproduction.* No identity covers the ABI, so
   `scripts/pool/v3/programs.mjs` (`check:pool:v3-programs`, in
   `check:scripts`) compiles the sources again and requires the shipped
   file byte for byte. A wrong ABI could only make proving fail: the prover
   checks a proof's public inputs against the task's and verifies it before
   returning.
4. *Declared identities are required.* `ProofCheck` keeps only `verify` and
   `parallel`, for transition checks. Readers, the journal and the wallet take
   a `DeclaredVerifier`, whose identities `requireConfigurationVerifier`
   requires to be the configuration's six, undefined refused as a setup
   error (TypeError). The scope walk checks this on every read. The per-object
   verifier name and the wallet's and journal's in-memory read fallback are
   removed: their reads always keep state in `<path>.replay` and
   `<path>.reads`.
5. Report source closures follow a `src/` module's data files read by URL,
   so a report binding the prover binds `programs.json` too.

**Rationale:**
- *Compile at install or at first use* would ship the Noir compiler (a
  devDependency) to every party, and trust each machine's compile to equal the
  manifest. A shipped file checked by identity needs neither.
- *A generated TypeScript module* holding the artifacts would load without
  file I/O but mixes 90 KB of data into the source tree's code and its
  declarations. A JSON import would share one mutable module object between
  callers. A fresh read per call gives each caller its own copy.
- *Ship the verification keys too*, so a verify-only party derives nothing
  and needs no G1 file (Next 4 (j)), is not taken here: it changes what a
  reader loads and what memory it needs, which the reader command's sizing
  (M5b.6) decides.
- *Keep undeclared verifiers for tests* keeps a second naming mechanism and
  an in-memory path no installed party uses. Stub verifiers now declare the
  adopted identities, as the stubs that exercised the kept path already did.

**Evidence:** `test/pool-v3-programs.test.ts` (the shipped set, fields,
compiler, canonical bytecode, swapped relations, unreadable file),
`test/pool-v3-verifier.test.ts` (a derived key or bytecode not the

## 2026-10-02 — Close five deferred review findings: kept force, testnet anchor, side-branch quota, served lag and adopted receipts (M9c2)

**Status:** accepted (slice 9, M9c2; reader, Ergo venue and operator store; no
specification change).

**Question:** five findings deferred from earlier reviews (WORK.md Next 4):
(q) `forces` reused a kept publication verdict without its snapshot
dependency (pool-v3 §14 requires the evidence that grounded a kept class and
its dependencies); (a) the guard accepted a testnet-context profile anchored on
a mainnet header; (e) ErgoVenue's side-branch quota never reset; (b) `package()`
served a published commitment the venue never held after the lag (C2.4.3); (c)
`submit` could return a lapsed tail's receipt for a statement later adopted by
force (pool-v3 §7.2, C2b.4.1–2).

**Decision:**
1. **(q)** A kept publication verdict is reused only after its snapshot (the
   backing's latest valid checkpoint strictly before its index) is classified
   on this read's evidence, by the code a fresh classification runs; a kept
   verdict whose shape that snapshot rules out (force where the publication
   cannot force, or a refusal without a check where it can) is kept state that
   does not match, so the read discards it and classifies again. The finding
   was not reachable: every caller of `forces` already classified those
   checkpoints first (an opening's parents, the clock's final snapshot read in
   `around` and in receipt reads). The change makes the dependency local
   instead of resting on call order.
2. **(a)** `ErgoVenue` reads the testnet reference context only above an
   anchor whose difficulty is below mainnet's `initialDifficultyHex`
   (1,199,990,374,400), the counterpart of the synthetic context's
   difficulty-1 anchor. Within an epoch the testnet rules require the parent's
   difficulty, so a mainnet anchor was followed, with mainnet's real work, up
   to its next epoch boundary (at most 127 headers). Rejected: a network field
   (headers carry none); a known testnet checkpoint (the 1,025-header context
   cannot reach one, and checkpoints were left out on 2026-09-27); re-deriving
   an epoch-boundary difficulty under testnet rules (needs 128 more context
   headers and changes `ergoAnchorContext`).
3. **(e)** Each side header charges its supplier 16 heights, and every height
   the best chain advances since the supplier's last charge forgives one; the
   credited height never falls, so a heavier, shorter chain credits nothing
   twice. Outstanding side headers stay below the quota plus one sync's header
   budget, and over any stretch a supplier adds at most one more per 16 heights
   of best-chain advance, which is the network's work. Rejected: reset on
   adoption (serve the best chain once and reset for free), per sync (repeats
   the header budget; memory unbounded), on finality passing the fork (an old
   deep branch is forgiven at once), a fixed window (double bursts at edges),
   one height per header (a supplier keeping pace is never cut off). This
   replaces the lifetime quota of decisions/2026-09.md.
4. **(b)** A published commitment counts as served only while
   `now < at + lag` from its signing index, the in-flight window `ready()`
   already uses (C2.4.4); after it the latest held own commitment is served,
   and a commitment the venue includes later is served again (C2.3.5).
5. **(c)** Adoption replaces the stored receipt of a statement that a lapsed
   tail had admitted, so exact resubmission, or a re-proof, returns the
   adopting segment's receipt. pool-v3 §7.2's "original receipt" is read as the
   receipt of the statement's live admission: its adoption paragraph gives the
   adopted statement a new receipt, and the tail's receipt reads lapsed
   (C2b.4.3). Only adoption can meet an existing row: `submit` answers a known
   statement first, and force refuses a recovery statement already effective
   (REPEATED_STATEMENT, imports included). The tail receipt stays in the
   command log.

**Evidence:** one regression test per item; each fails on the pre-fix

## 2026-10-01 — Read C3.8 per witnessed index from the record a frontier read holds, and tell a taken release apart at force

**Status:** accepted (slice 9, M9c1; reader, force check order and wallet; no
specification change).

**Question:** C3.8 says what the record shows of a demand: dishonour past its
deadline with no settlement, unless a timely acceptance stood unreleased (the
holder's lapse); a void by a spent tag from the index the spend was witnessed
at, prospective, with the earlier indices "read there as it stood"; a release
taken by another demand's settlement reading as released. No v3 code read it,
the backer had no way to publish its acceptance (C3.4), and a release refused
for its output was reported `OUTPUT` before the recovery checks ran, so a
reader could not tell whether it "met every condition of C2b.3.2 at its index
except" its output. The text leaves three readings open: when a withdrawal or
a settlement witnessed after the deadline counts, which witnessed acceptances
count, and what "witnessed" means for an event in history.

**Decision:**
1. **One reading per index.** The reading at any index is the record through
   that index. A statement in canonical history counts from the earliest
   canonical checkpoint holding it, which is the index replay judged it at
   (a continuation keeps its prefix's indices; an adopted record its
   publication's); a publication with force from its own index. A demand ends
   at the earliest of its settlement, its withdrawal or a void, and every end
   is prospective: past the deadline and before its end the demand reads as
   the backer's dishonour or the holder's lapse, and a later end does not erase
   it. This is C3.8's void rule applied to the two other ends, Construction
   §C3's "claims still live past the deadline are the backer's visible
   failure", and the latch Extensions' trigger reads ("a withdrawn or voided
   demand counts at the indices it actually stood unanswered"). A withdrawal
   witnessed at or before the deadline leaves no failure; one after it keeps
   the dishonour recorded before it. Ends at one index are ordered as the
   record orders them: history by position, then the venue's order. A demand
   first witnessed at or after its own deadline reads neither dishonour nor
   lapse: C3.3 requires the deadline strictly ahead of the index the demand
   is witnessed at, and Construction §C3 calls a deadline no acceptance could
   precede a manufactured verdict; without this, an operator admitting a
   demand at its door and checkpointing it late would record a dishonour K
   could not have answered.
2. **Acceptances are what the venue witnessed.** An acceptance counts where a
   venue publication carries it, alone (publication kind 2) or in a release
   (kind 3), routed to the demand's backing, verifying strictly under that
   backing's K and due no later than the demand; it is placed at the first
   index any copy was witnessed and is timely where its deadline is later than
   that index by more than the lag (C3.4). The past-deadline reading is the
   holder's lapse where a timely acceptance was not taken, else dishonour.
   Every timely acceptance, and any release that takes it, is witnessed by the
   demand's deadline, so that reading is the same at every index past it.
3. **Force judges new nullifiers and outputs last**, in replay's order (context,
   backing, proof, anchors, recovery, then uniqueness), and a release whose
   output exists as a settlement output of another demand, in the snapshot or
   forced earlier, is refused `TAKEN` instead of `OUTPUT`. Force verdicts are
   unchanged; only the reported check of a publication failing two conditions
   changes. Replay state keeps each output's inserting settlement already, so
   C3.8's "demand of the settlement that inserted it" costs no column; the
   force overlay keeps it for forced settlements. Kept replay layout 5 discards
   publication verdicts kept under the old order.
4. **The wallet** reads it: `presentation(demand, package, terms)` returns the
   reading at the venue's index for any party, and `publishAcceptance(alias,
   publisher)` publishes the backer's saved acceptance (wallet profile
   `moe/wallet/v3/7`, which keeps the acceptance's backing to route it). A
   frontier read's `answers` lists every acceptance and release of the
   backing with the release's verdict; the disclosure count reads the
   releases without force among them.

**Rationale:** alternatives for (1): *the reading at the judging index alone*
(settled, withdrawn or voided means never dishonoured) lets a holder erase a
recorded dishonour by withdrawing, which C3.8 refuses for the void and
Extensions' latch refuses for both; *a settlement erasing earlier indices*
would make the record at an index depend on a checkpoint not yet witnessed,
so two readers judging the same index from the same record would differ by
when they read. The cost falls on a backer whose operator witnesses an
admitted settlement or withdrawal after the deadline. For a settlement,
publishing its acceptance when it accepts (C3.4) turns those indices into
the holder's lapse, as the trigger reads them; a withdrawal admitted in time
and witnessed late leaves them as dishonour. The record cannot show an
admission index, only the index the operator's checkpoint was witnessed at,
and the operator is the one K's terms declare (or K's replacement rule
appoints), so its commitment cadence is the backing's own. A deadline beyond
the declared silence duration bounds it; without a silence clause nothing
does. The converse holds too: an operator that checkpoints a demand only at
or after its deadline records no dishonour, which is a refusal of service the
non-service count (C2b.5.2) still reads while it lasts. A deadline only just
ahead of the demand's witnessing (less than the lag plus one) leaves K no
timely acceptance, the holder's short window that Construction §C3 calls
worthless evidence; a stricter cut-off is the trigger's answer window, an
Extensions term. For (2): *published acceptances only* would read the acceptance a
holder's own release carries as unwitnessed and its non-release as the
backer's failure, though K's signature is on the venue; anyone can republish
an acceptance it learned, which can only turn a dishonour into a lapse the
acceptance's existence already justified. For (3): *re-judging an `OUTPUT`
refusal* in the reader would run the recovery checks twice in two places;
*a new column for the inserting demand* would duplicate the settlement record
the event row keeps. Not done: a supply reader's list of every dishonoured
demand of a backing (slice 10's reader command can build it from the same
function over the standing-demand record).

**Evidence:** `test/pool-v3-redemption.test.ts` through wallet operations on
the local venue with stand-in proofs: an unanswered demand reads dishonour from
the index after its deadline, kept through a later withdrawal; a timely
published acceptance left unreleased reads as the holder's lapse; a settlement
and a withdrawal in time read with no failure; an acceptance K did not sign,
one due after the demand, one of another demand, one routed to another backing
and one witnessed with only the lag left count nothing; a spend of the note
after the deadline voids the demand from its checkpoint, keeping the
dishonour before it; in a gap, a settlement of another demand under an
acceptance K signed naming the same owner takes the holder's release

forced settlement and from one in the snapshot's history, `OUTPUT` from a

`test/pool-v3-scope-wallet.test.ts`: in a shared segment a demand of one

A fresh adversarial review found no blocker and confirmed the force verdicts

a payment fails only where a reserved input is spent otherwise, and a view

settlement. The presenter key derives from the seed and the notice (tags,
instant, deadline), but replay state kept no instant. The plan also had
`reprove` rebuild acts whose segment ended, beside payments.

**Decision:**
1. Replay state keeps each standing demand's instant (its public input), so the
   wallet recognizes its own demands by deriving the presenter key; `sync`
   lists them, and `withdraw(alias, id)` and `settle(alias, acceptance)` read
   the notice from the view, naming the demand by its identity (a settlement
   by the acceptance, which names it). Withdrawal and settlement need the
   demand standing anyway, so the view is the one source of the notice for a
   saved demand and a found one alike. Replay layout 4, journal profile
   `pool-store/v3/4`, wallet profile `moe/wallet/v3/6`.
2. A demand reserves its notes only while prepared. Once it stands, its lock
   (C3.7) holds them until it ends, whichever copy saved the act ending it, or
   its deadline passes. The earlier rule kept them reserved until this
   wallet's own withdrawal was final, so a withdrawal by a restored copy, a
   settlement or a lapse never freed them. A prepared settlement reserves the
   demand's notes, over any other reservation: the demand's lock refuses any
   other spend at the door while it stands, and its admission fails the rest.
   A payment or act is built only from a view at least as recent as every view
   the wallet synced or fulfilled at, or a saved record was built or decided at, and an older
   view fails no record.
3. An act whose segment ended fails and is made again under a new alias;
   `reprove` stays the payments'. C2.10.8 gives a rebuilt statement a new
   identity and fresh evidence: a new proof, a new release or withdrawal
   signature, a new issue signature from K, a settlement output derived for the
   new segment, and a demand whose instant must again fall in C3.3's window.
   What a reproof keeps for a payment, the outputs a payee was given (C1.2.5),
   an act has no counterpart for: an issue's output is the request's under any
   alias (its intent binds it), a burn's change is the wallet's own. The
   acceptance stands, as C2.10.8 says.

**Rationale:** alternatives for finding demands: *reading the demand's record
from the wallet's evidence file* needs a lookup by position per segment and a
second path for forced demands held as venue answers; *a hook in the replay
that saves found demands* loses one once kept state moves past its record if
the wallet's write fails; *a presenter derivation without the instant* would
give two demands over the same notes and deadline one key, against C3.3's fresh
key per demand. One column of local state costs the replay and journal layout
changes and nothing in public bytes. For acts, a reproof path per kind (issue
re-signed by K, settlement re-derived, demand re-windowed) would add a second
mechanism that ends where acting again under a new alias already ends.

**Evidence:** `test/pool-v3-redemption.test.ts`: a demand found from the seed
under service and settled into the record the lost wallet makes; another seed
refused (`UNKNOWN`); a forced gap demand found and withdrawn from the seed,
freeing the lost wallet's notes; after an operator succession, a settlement
of the ended segment failed and a restored wallet's new settlement under the
same acceptance in the successor's segment final; notes free at the deadline
without a withdrawal; a view older than the records builds nothing
(`CHANGED_VIEW`) and fails no record across a succession; a prepared
settlement reserves its notes and takes them over a payment prepared before
the demand stood. A fresh adversarial review found no blocker. Its two major
findings, both local accounting with protocol safety intact, are fixed as
above: a settlement reserved nothing, so a stale view (or a lapse unread) let a
burn take a note a prepared settlement needed; and a stale view failed a live
act as of a dead segment and freed its inputs. Its minor findings are fixed
(intents bind the backing, a settle alias conflict is tested, the guide says a
void demand is not listed); a read-back found the stale-view guard blind to
views a wallet only synced, so the wallet keeps the newest index it synced
or fulfilled at, written in that operation's own commit; a reservation conflict refusing a settlement was
proposed and not taken, since it would block a settlement behind a payment the
lock already holds back. Not covered: a scoped backing's demand refused as
another backing's, real proofs. Unit suite and `check:scripts` pass; stand-in
proofs only.

**Spec change:** none needed.

## 2026-10-01 — Adopt pool-v3 with one configuration and hold its manifest in the runtime (M8a)

**Status:** accepted (slice 8 M8a; spec e7f7f24).

**Question:** slice 8 ordered adoption last, after every rule that could move
configuration bytes was settled
([order](2026-09.md#2026-09-29--order-configuration-adoption-and-state-its-proving-parameters)):
M1–M3 fixed the parameters, their loader and the sources' text, M4–M6 the
certificates, lifetime evidence and settlement pre-emption without a relation
change, and M7 the one-transaction condition. What must adoption say, and
what must the runtime change?

**Decision:**
1. *The specification adopts the candidate's identities unchanged.* Pool-v3
   §11.4 names configHash `7ddbb7e8…a618`, the twelve bytecode and key
   identities, the helper, the eight source hashes at reference-ts
   `103004e`, the compiler used (Noir `1.0.0-beta.26`), the pinned backend
   (bb.js 5.2.0) and verifier target, the parameters' layout and the
   14,656-byte proof length. No configuration byte changes, so every
   fixture, domain and report identity stays valid.
2. *The construction is fixed by its name, as v1 and v2 are* (§1). A change
   to its bytes, identities or verdict rules, or to an instantiated
   contract's rule as v3 reads it, is `moe/pool/v4`; v3 reads the contracts'
   text of the adopting revision, and later edits may only correct text
   without changing a byte, identity or verdict. venue-ergo §1: its context
   names its rules as at adoption, and a change to attribution, index or
   answers is a new context, hence a new venue. Stated limits are adopted
   with the rules, notably C3.8's refusal-of-service residual.
3. *The runtime holds the manifest.* `POOL_V3_MANIFEST` in
   `src/pool/v3/configuration.ts` holds the toolchain, verifier target,
   sources, identities, BN254 parameters (formerly `BN254_PARAMETERS` in
   `proof-verifier.ts`) and `PROOF_BYTES`. `adoptedConfiguration()`,
   `adoptedConfigurationBytes()` and `adoptedDomain()` derive the rest. The
   journal, readers, wallet and prover take no configuration: the option is
   removed, a verifier's declared identities are checked against the
   adopted ones, and a served preimage must equal the adopted bytes.
   `scripts/pool/v3/manifest.mjs` replaces `candidate.mjs` and
   `candidate-manifest.json`, checking the same manifest against the
   installed toolchain, the sources and each build; the conformance check
   also asserts that its derived identities frame §11.4's hash.
4. *The guard stays, with a new reason.* Adoption is not a release: the
   runtime refuses mainnet until the production gates close under separate
   authority. `CandidateVenueError` and `CANDIDATE_CONTEXTS` become
   `ReferenceVenueError` and `REFERENCE_CONTEXTS`.

**Alternatives:**
- *Adopt in the specification only*, keeping the caller-supplied
  configuration. That keeps a selection mechanism no party needs, and
  leaves a misconfigured caller free to run another domain.
- *Ship compiled artifacts and load them in the runtime*, removing the
  `programs` argument too. That is packaging, which slice 9's installable
  commands own; it does not change what is adopted.
- *Let later edits refine verdicts in place*, for example to settle C3.8's
  refusal-of-service residual. Holders and readers of a declared backing
  would then judge the same evidence differently by edition; a successor
  version keeps one verdict per name, as v1 and v2 already do.
- *Make 14,656 bytes a normative length rule.* M7 refused that tightening;
  §11.4 states the length as the pinned backend's.

**Evidence and limits:** an independent review recomputed every identity and
the configHash from the frame, both by the codec and by hand. It matched the
source hashes at `103004e` and on main against the manifest and the CI
conformance report, and found every prerequisite the old §1 and §11.1 listed
met. It found one blocker, a §8 clause still saying adoption was unset, and
material points: the missing versioning rule, "selects them by holding them"
reading as if no check were required, venue-ergo's wording implying Ergo was
mandatory, then on read-back the unpinned contract text and venue-ergo's
same-context edits. All are resolved in e7f7f24. A second independent review
of the runtime found no blocker. It found one material gap, now closed: the
loopback service client still took the expected domain from its caller and
now uses the adopted one. Its minor points are applied: the conformance check
compares `notes.nr` with the manifest too, a served preimage is compared as
bytes, and stale "candidate" text is fixed. Declaring verifier identities
stays optional for a test double, so a caller's `verify` with other keys is
bound to the adopted keys only when it declares them. That predates this
change, and slice 9 requires declared identities. Unit tests cover the
adopted hash, fresh copies, a frozen manifest and a backup sealed under
another domain. Tests that used a synthetic configuration now use the
adopted one, with stand-in verifiers as before. Every current report is
re-recorded from CI. The live two-backing drill is M8b.

## 2026-10-01 — Check the one-transaction condition in the guard and fill Ergo boxes at their real output index

**Status:** accepted (slice 8 M7).

**Question:** venue-ergo §8 makes a pool-v3 configuration publishable on an
Ergo venue only where its largest publication fits one transaction at the
venue's kind-4 location
([condition](../decisions/2026-09.md#2026-09-15--a-configurations-publications-fit-one-ergo-transaction)).
Slice 8's M7 was to check it against the configuration. Three gaps stood. No
code knew the configuration's largest publication: the record codec and the
verifier admit proofs up to 131,072 bytes, and 14,656 was an observation.
§8's capacity, a 3,981-byte piece and 24 pieces of 95,544 bytes, came from a
retired probe that counted the output index as two bytes and only whole
pieces. And the publisher reserved a three-byte index, so its pieces held
3,980 bytes and a 95,544-byte publication needed 25 outputs that did not fit.
Nothing refused a venue whose kind-4 location could not carry a release.

**Decision:**
1. *The backend fixes the largest publication.* A probe proved a valid issue
   proof and changed its length: a word appended (zero or the last word
   repeated), the last word removed, a word prefixed, the proof doubled. Pinned
   bb.js 5.2.0 answered false to each, and so did the shared verifier.
   `PROOF_BYTES` (14,656) is now a configuration constant. The conformance
   check asserts every relation's proof has that length and that the issue
   proof with a word appended, its last word repeated or removed is refused
   by both. Only the issue key is tried; an off-length publication of
   another relation is invalid anyway, so it need not fit. `publicationBound(proofBytes)` derives the longest
   publication from the codec's body bounds: 131,914 at §5's limit, which is
   §13.1's frame ceiling, and 15,498 at `PROOF_BYTES`, a release.
2. *Pieces fill boxes at their real index.* The pinned node checks
   `out.bytes.length <= MaxBoxSize` (4,096) over the box with its transaction
   id and index, a one-byte VLQ below 128 (`ErgoTransaction.scala` at
   v6.0.6). A run that fits one transaction has about 25 outputs, so the
   publisher sizes pieces at a one-byte index. A pay-to-public-key piece box
   is 4,096 bytes holding 3,982 at current heights, and the last piece takes
   the room left.
3. *Inputs take only the room the outputs leave.* `inputRoom` counts the
   record's outputs, change of the largest value a box holds and the fee. It
   allows as many 90-byte signed inputs as fit, at most 64. So a fresh build
   of a record within capacity is never refused for the transaction's size;
   it is refused for funding when one box cannot pay for it, which is the
   publisher's own state. A rebuild on the same inputs at a height whose VLQ
   is a byte longer (past 2²¹ or 2²⁸) can find less room than the inputs it
   must keep. It is then refused and the old transaction is kept, the only
   one the record has, as before this change.
4. *`ergoRunCarries(location, length)`* holds where one transaction with
   one input carries the record at the location at every height (u32), fee
   and value (u64) and per-byte minimum the publisher accepts (up to 10⁶);
   `ergoRunCapacity` is the longest such record. With every option at its
   longest encoding, a record of that length builds with one input and one
   byte more is refused before anything is remembered or sent; under the
   defaults at a current height the publisher builds up to 96,021 bytes, since
   it checks the room it has, not the floor. The floor is 95,910 bytes at a
   pay-to-public-key location (proofs up to 95,040 bytes), less for a longer
   tree, and below 15,498 past a 3,368-byte tree.
5. *The v3 guard checks the condition.* `referenceVenue` refuses an Ergo
   reference profile whose kind-4 location cannot carry
   `publicationBound(PROOF_BYTES)`, naming the condition. Every v3 entry point
   reaches the guard, and so will the adopted context at M8. It evaluates
   `ergoRunCarries` once, a few milliseconds, with no cache.
6. *The fee stays the deployment's.* The pinned node admits any fee of at
   least `minimalFeeAmount`, and both relay paths refuse only a transaction
   longer than 98,304 bytes (`TransactionsApiRoute`, `ErgoNodeViewSynchronizer`).
   Its default `mempoolSorting = "random"` does not order at random: each node
   picks fee per byte or fee per execution cost (`NodeConfigurationSettings`).
   So a long publication at the default fee ranks below short transactions on
   about half the nodes once a pool or block fills. Admission is unaffected,
   and the live run was mined at the default fee. A size-proportional fee
   against C3 deadlines under congestion is recorded as a follow-up, not built here.

**Alternatives:**
- *Restate §8 at the publisher's old numbers* (a 3,980-byte piece). This keeps
  a reservation that no transaction within the limit needs, and §8's
  capacity would still be wrong.
- *Bound the verifier's proofs at 14,656* instead of naming the length. The
  backend already refuses other lengths. Tightening would change a §5 bound
  that the codec shares, for a cheap denial that §14's budgets already cover.
- *Check the condition only at adoption.* That would leave a deployment free
  to pick a kind-4 tree that cannot carry a release. The guard is the one
  place where the configuration meets a venue.
- *Size capacity for 64 inputs.* That would spend about 5.8 KB of every run
  on a case that funding consolidation avoids. Bounding inputs by room keeps
  §8's number the network's.

**Evidence and limits:** unit tests cover full 4,096-byte piece boxes, a
record at capacity under every option at its longest encoding against one
byte more, input room taking one box where two would not fit, the guard's
threshold tree, the guard's refusal, a saved state of the previous layout
refused by its version, and the publication bound against encoded bytes. On the
testnet the own v6.0.6 node checked and accepted a 95,910-byte run as one
98,190-byte transaction: 24 piece boxes of 4,096 bytes holding 3,982 each and
one of 342. It was mined at 574,731 beside the three plain records, and the
reader reassembled it exactly from the root-checked section
([report](../docs/ergo-publisher-verification.json)). Beyond that run,
capacity is a serialization bound: other relay policies and miners are outside it.
An independent review reproduced every number. It checked monotonicity over every
length, 864 option combinations at VLQ boundaries and 162 real builds, and found
no blocker or material defect. Its minor findings are fixed above: the
same-inputs rebuild's room, the saved-state version (now 3), the overstated
refusal at capacity, the mempool ordering, the relay limit's citation, which
variants the check asserts, and a single evaluation in place of a cached bisection.

**Spec change:** [venue-ergo §8 at 01f922c](https://github.com/mediumofexchange/money-from-first-principles/blob/01f922c/venue-ergo.md#8-publishing).

## 2026-10-01 — Keep an Ergo index witnessed while the best chain keeps its block, and replace a publication only on a supplier's answer

**Status:** accepted (rolling audit of area 28, the venue core and the Ergo
runtime venue and publisher; PR on branch `fix/area28-venue-audit`).

**Question:** the audit found two places where the Ergo runtime departed from
what its rules promise:
- *The clock.* venue-ergo §2 derived the witnessed index from the current
  best tip. A heavier chain can be shorter where it crosses an epoch boundary
  at a higher required difficulty. `ErgoVenue.sync` kept its clock while the
  best chain kept the witnessed block, but `restore` re-derived it from the
  tip, so a durable view could not reopen.
- *The publisher.* It promised one transaction per record, but built a second,
  non-conflicting one after a lost reply and an outage across a height change.
  Its settlement judged landing by record and change boxes. Swept or chained
  publications therefore kept their inputs reserved for good, and an outage
  during a settle forgot landed change. One slow or lying supplier cost a
  timeout per invented box and per unsettled ancestor.

What should each rule be?

**Decision:**
1. *An index stays witnessed while the best chain keeps its block* (venue-ergo
   §2). A chain that leaves the block is a reorganization deeper than the
   depth, the venue's failure, as before.
   - Sync and restore check the same thing. Restore also requires a kept
     header that buried the block at the depth, so the clock is reproducible
     from retained headers.
   - A shorter best chain's next block can fall inside the lag of the kept
     clock, so `publishRecord` refuses until the chain is again as long as
     the clock's depth. The publisher asks the same in its own turn and before
     each transaction it sends, so one queued meanwhile waits too. C2.3.5's
     bound then holds for every record the view sends, signed at or below its
     clock.
   - A failure of the view's own mid-sync leaves it failed, in memory too.
2. *A publication is replaced only after a supplier answers that it lacks the
   transaction*, never while no supplier answers.
   - A refused transaction is not replaced while a supplier holds a
     transaction the record had before.
   - A replacement never spends a replaced transaction's change, and one that
     would be the same transaction is not made.
   - A record has at most eight transactions.
   - A node says it lacks a transaction only where its mempool answered and
     its index has read every block it holds (`/blockchain/indexedHeight`,
     confirmed in the v6.0.6 jar). A mined transaction leaves the mempool
     before the index reads its block, so an earlier "not found" let an
     honest node trigger a replacement.
   - The saved state (layout 2) keeps the replaced ids, and a layout-1 state
     is refused.
3. *Settling asks no supplier.* A publication whose record the view holds is
   forgotten.
   - Its inputs are released: whatever spends them next conflicts with a
     transaction that did not land.
   - Its change stays the publisher's own until a build finds it gone.
   - Reservations are derived from the unsettled publications, not stored.
4. *Suppliers are asked at once.*
   - A box is denied at the first answer that the supplier lacks it.
   - Ancestry is walked only where `hasTransaction` answers false. The
     ancestry is known locally: the prefix a supplier holds is found by
     bisection and the rest is sent oldest first. Each supplier's walk ends at
     one deadline of two timeouts, so every attempt keeps what it sent.
   - A record is not replaced while some transaction it had before has no
     supplier saying it lacks it.
   - Offered boxes are checked a batch at a time, after the publisher's own
     change.
   - Every node body is bounded as it streams (`nodeText`).
5. *The view indexes the records it holds* by their first index, so `holds` is
   a lookup.
   - A section answer is read by index over its own length, capped in count
     and bytes.
   - The supplier of the last section is asked first.

**Rationale:**
- *Clock.* A clock that moves back with no change of blocks would withdraw
  answers a reader had given. It would also judge a crossing of a block once
  buried at the depth by the shorter chain's depth. The alternative, answering
  only up to the current tip's final index while keeping the pin for failure
  detection, has both faults. `RecordVenue`, `FixtureVenue` and the v3 journal's
  kept windows already assume a monotone clock. Two readers that saw different
  chains already differ in their clocks; neither ever answers different bytes
  for one witnessed index.
- *The lag.* A kept clock on a shorter chain would let an act signed at it
  land inside its lag, with no force under C3.3. A second, signing clock beside
  the answering one would fix that with a mechanism. Waiting does it without
  one: the wait is at most the depth's blocks, and only after a heavier,
  shorter branch. Such a branch needs real work at an epoch boundary carrying a
  timestamp honest nodes refuse.
- *Replacement.* The supplier whose answer is missing is the only one that
  could show the old transaction, so building without an answer is what
  produced the second transaction.
  - Tracking the replaced ids closes the lagging-supplier variant: the
    replacement is refused because the first transaction spent its input.
  - The cap bounds what a supplier that denies real inputs can cost: at most
    seven extra fees for a record, where before every attempt could cost one.
  - The residual case stays documented: a supplier that denies a live
    transaction's input and does not show the transaction.
- *Settlement.* Landing matters only for change.
  - Keeping change that never existed costs one refused attempt, after which a
    build finds it gone.
  - Forgetting change that did land loses it wherever an index lags.
  - Releasing inputs makes the reserved set derivable and bounded by unsettled
    publications. It was growing for the deployment's life (200 entries after
    400 publications in the reviewer's probe).
- *Cost.* Measured on the reviewer's probes:
  - 500 invented boxes cost about 820 supplier timeouts, and 200 unsettled
    ancestors about 325.
  - A settle at 10⁵ indices with 1,024 pending publications took 19.7 s,
    scanning every index for each.
  - The regression tests bound each of these to a timeout or two, or to a
    lookup.

**Evidence:**
- `test/ergo-publisher.test.ts`, "replacement, settlement and supplier cost"
  and the node-body bound.
- `test/ergo-store.test.ts`, for a heavier, shorter chain. It mines real
  Autolykos v2 work on a low-difficulty mainnet-context anchor
  (`test/ergo-chain.ts` `WorkedChain`), since difficulty 1 cannot show it.
- `test/ergo-venue.test.ts`, for section intake, supplier order and policy.
- `test/neutral-core.test.ts`, for `FixtureVenue` intake and read-once venue
  records.
- `scripts/pool/v3/check.mjs`, for the verifier's own copies on real proofs.

Each new case fails on the sources before the fix.

**Review.** One independent reviewer read the integrated patch and found no
blockers and four material findings, all fixed in it:
- the kept clock broke the lag, so publication now waits;
- restore took an unburied clock, so a burial header is now required;
- a slow "lacks it" answer still cost two timeouts per ancestor, so each walk
  now has a deadline;
- an honest node's lagging index read as "lacks it", so the index height is
  now checked.

It also raised minor items, likewise fixed:
- a memory view stuck after its own failure;
- the fixed anchor-context timeout;
- the publisher timer cap;
- a real-proof case that did not distinguish old from new;
- identical height rebuilds;
- a loose timing test;
- the undocumented attempt cap;
- unread node bodies left open.

A read-back by a second fresh reviewer found one material regression: under
the first deadline, a walk that descended one ancestor at a time made no lasting
progress once descending took longer than the deadline. Bisecting the ancestry
and sending it oldest first fixed that. The read-back also found three minor
items, all fixed:
- a publication queued before the chain shortened was sent anyway, so
  readiness is now checked in the publisher's turn;
- a replaced transaction whose fate was unknown still allowed a rebuild;
- `holds` was checked after the wait.

Deferred items are in WORK.md: the Ergo view's memory and per-sync work grow
with chain age, and so does the durable journal.

## 2026-10-01 — Add no certificate encoding, keep §12 to the kinds a reader reads, and prune no retained evidence (M4)

**Status:** accepted (slice 8 M4; specification only, no runtime change).

**Question:** pool-v3 §1 listed a "complete-certificate encoding" among the
adoption prerequisites. Slice 8's M4 was to fit certificates, and the question
of whether §12's kind-11 venue evidence needs Ergo bytes, to the retention
that §14 fixed in M5
([order](../decisions/2026-09.md#2026-09-29--order-configuration-adoption-and-state-its-proving-parameters)).
The runtime reads packages of kinds 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 10 and refuses a
package that carries 5, 8, 9 or 11 (`evidence-store.ts` `READ_KINDS`). Yet §12
assigned all four, and §12.1 accepted a terms field "as a kind-8 item". WORK.md's
Next also opened with pruning retained evidence that no read used, a limit
the [storage decision](../decisions/2026-09.md#2026-09-29--keep-replay-state-in-each-partys-sqlite-storage-committed-at-keep-points)
left open. What must
adoption fix, and should a reader prune?

**Decision:**
1. *No certificate encoding.* A verdict's certificate is its closure: the
   objects §12.1 derives from the read and the existing rules, and the venue
   answers the reader's own verifier establishes. To show a verdict, a reader
   hands over a package of the closure, which it must still hold
   (C2.10.13). The receiver derives the same dependencies, reads the venue
   itself and judges. C2.10.13's stranger is handed the excluded verdict's
   closure; §9.1's compact intrinsic exclusion, the only compact replacement,
   stands in for the target's trail where it applies. C2.10.13's smaller set
   shows the operator's provable fault, not the verdict. C4.6's restoration
   package is likewise a package of the closure. The venue evidence behind it
   stays with the reader's verifier and the venue's suppliers.
2. *Kinds 5, 8, 9 and 11 are unassigned* and are not reassigned in v3.
   - A header with its scoped terms travels as a trail of count zero (§10
     allows n = 0). Terms resolve from any trail of the segment.
   - Publications and venue evidence reach a reader only through its own
     venue verifier, whose profile authenticates the evidence whatever
     supplies it (venue-ergo §§3–4), within §3's light-client limit.
3. *Retained evidence is not pruned by use.* Removing a supplier's junk is
   left to the party: it stops taking from that supplier. Where a file must
   shrink, it syncs a new file from another supplier, checks that the new
   file's reads resolve, and only then replaces the old one. The evidence file
   may be the holder's only copy ([wallet guide](../docs/POOL_V3_WALLET.md)),
   so it is never removed first.

**Rationale:**
- *Certificate alternatives.*
  - A dependency list or graph serialized beside the objects would duplicate
    §12.1's derivation: a reader must derive the dependencies anyway, and a
    listed edge authenticates nothing.
  - An asserted or signed verdict replaces evidence with a party's word: a
    cached verdict is not a certificate (C2.10.13).
  - Packaged venue evidence (for Ergo, headers and block sections in kind 11)
    would be a second path to the record. The profile already accepts any
    supplier, and the record ranges §13.3 reads from index zero are about
    4.2 GB of sections at the design point. It would add bytes, not evidence.
  - A compact certificate for a reader that does not replay needs a succinct
    relation, which §14 rejected; Construction invariant 23 keeps the spent
    set's non-membership for one later.
- *Kinds.* Kinds 5 and 8 duplicated a trail of count zero, which costs 29
  framing bytes, plus 68 for each field it leaves empty. The runtime never read
  either kind. Its store keeps a trail's head apart from its records, so a
  lapse read needs no records. One narrowing: §12.1 resolved a terms field from
  "a trail or a kind-8 item", and now resolves it from a trail of the segment,
  as the runtime always did. A field that one trail lacks comes from a
  count-zero trail of the same segment.
- *Pruning.* What a party needs later is not what its reads have used so far,
  and the incremental supply relies on what the store holds:
  - Honest suppliers send only what commitments name. The journal serves each
    directory and all of its entries' snapshots from the requested sequence
    through its selection, what openings took, and, for each segment those
    snapshots name, one trail through its furthest named record (`store.ts`
    `parts`). Forked and excluded trails are dependencies (C2.10.11–12). So
    an honest supplier sends nothing beyond what the operator's commitments
    name, though for one backing's reader that includes other backings'
    snapshots and segments. Anything else comes from a hostile supplier. The
    per-supply quota bounds it only weakly, at 64 GiB by default
    (`EVIDENCE_QUOTA.file`).
  - A read looks up the ranges of the backings it judges, including every
    scoped backing of each judged segment, not everything the operator's
    commitments name. A wallet later paid in a backing outside those scopes
    needs other directory entries and earlier ranges. Evidence supplied ahead
    of finality stays unused until the venue shows it.
  - `sync` asks after a sequence, and the journal serves each trail after a
    position the store holds. Pruning what the next part assumes makes
    assembly fail, and `sync` then asks again from nothing: a transfer linear
    in history, the first-sync cost. Avoiding that needs supplier marks rolled
    back to the last selection a read used.
  - Telling a hostile supplier's objects from evidence needs roots: the
    directories of every commitment the venue holds for each operator the
    party follows, over the venue's whole history, plus protection for
    evidence ahead of finality. That is a second walk beside the reads, kept
    only to remove what a hostile supplier adds.
  - Tagging each row with the supply that first brought it would let a party
    drop one supplier's contribution soundly: a removed dependency only
    leaves a read unresolved, and a full resupply restores it. That costs a
    column and a deletion path, for a case the replacement-file remedy covers.
- *Side paths recorded* in WORK.md: a per-supply bound tied to what the venue
  newly holds, which is the direct answer to hostile growth, and serving a
  reader only the segments its backing's checkpoints name. Today the journal
  serves every segment its directories name.

**Evidence:** source reading of `evidence-store.ts` (kinds, heads apart from
records, terms per segment), `store.ts` `parts`, `service-client.ts` `sync`
and `scope-reader.ts`'s descent. `local-check`'s claimed-range case, a package
carrying a kind-11 completeness claim, refuses `unsupported-scope`, as the new
text requires. `test/pool-v3-evidence-store.test.ts` refuses kinds 5, 8, 9
and 11. No code, report or runtime pin changes. The runtime pin stays, and
implementation status records the later revision. An independent review
approved with fixes. It found one blocker: §13.2 still assigned kind 11. Four
material findings followed:
- a count-zero trail serves every checkpoint at n = 0, not only the opening;
- the stranger is handed the closure, with §9.1 only where it applies;
- C4.6's restoration package names its venue part;
- the draft's remedy removed what may be the holder's only copy.

All five are fixed, as are the minor findings: wording on how venue-ergo
authenticates evidence, why kind 2 stays, the cost of empty fields, the
decision's factual claims, and stale lines in pool-fault and here. The review
found no simpler sound pruning design. It suggested tagging rows with their
supply, recorded above.

**Spec change:** [pool-v3 §§1, 7–13 and pool-fault §§8–9](https://github.com/mediumofexchange/money-from-first-principles/commit/5584732).

## 2026-10-01 — Read a gap release taken by another demand's settlement as released

**Status:** accepted (dishonour reading over the pool; M6 of slice 8; no v3
reader of C3.8 exists yet).

**Question:** a settlement (C3.5) publishes its output's whole opening. A
backer that sees a release waiting at the venue in a gap can settle notes of
its own to the same `cm_out` first, under a demand and an acceptance of its
own naming the holder's `owner` and the holder's `rho_out`. Outputs must be
new, so the holder's release is refused `OUTPUT`, and C3.8 read the
acceptance that stood unreleased as the holder's lapse rather than the
backer's dishonour. A fresh `rho_out` per retry only asked the backer for
another prepared snapshot note. Under service with an honest operator the
backer cannot see a settle before its admission; a colluding operator can
refuse it outright, so a collision adds nothing there.

**Decision:** for the lapse reading, a release **taken** by another demand's
settlement means its acceptance does not stand unreleased, and it changes no
recovery state: a release witnessed at the venue that met every condition of
C2b.3.2 at its index except that its `cm_out` was already an output inserted
by a settlement of another demand (in the snapshot, or with force earlier in
venue order since the snapshot's adoption index). Equal outputs have equal
owners, and only K signs the acceptance that names an owner, so the taking
settlement shows K named this acceptance's owner for two demands; a backer
deriving each owner from its demand (pool-delivery C4.7) never does. C3.4
now requires distinct owners for distinct demands. A settle refused under
service, or an output created by a spend or an issue, takes no release. The
holder derives `rho_out` (C3.5) from its root secret, the input nullifiers,
the segment identity and a disclosure count read from the record (releases
of the demand bound to that segment witnessed without force), so a re-proof
after a release without force (a return or a commitment landing first) names
an output nobody has seen; a settlement re-proven under a new segment keeps
its nullifiers, not its output (pool-authority C2.10.8). Otherwise the
backer inserts a disclosed output first with an ordinary spend under
service, where no release can be taken.

**Rationale:** the reading needs no byte, relation, key or admission change.
Refused: (1) an acceptance naming `rho_out`, with the acceptance void once
its output exists — reviewed and blocked: anybody who learns the acceptance
can pay the backer the quantity into that output and turn the holder's lapse
into the backer's dishonour, at no net cost to a holder the backer already
paid in kind; voiding only on outputs the backer signs for reopens the
attack, since the backer then knows the output in advance and spends its own
notes to it. (2) A commitment tag of its own for settlement outputs: changes
every relation that authenticates a note, and their keys. (3) `rho_out`
fixed by the settlement's segment and first nullifier, checked in the clear:
a release left without force by a commitment landing first discloses an
output the live segment then admits from anybody, and the holder cannot
retry. (4) Counting any release refused for its output as released: a holder
can take its own release with a spend. (5) Admitting a repeated `cm_out`:
two leaves under one nullifier, a quantity nobody can spend or burn.

**Evidence:** `test/pool-v3-force.test.ts` shows the backer's own settlement
of another demand, naming the holder's owner and `rho_out`, taking the
output first in force, the holder's release refused `OUTPUT` with every
other check passing, and the two settlements' differing demands and equal
owner and output that the reading uses. Independent review blocked the first
proposal (alternative 1); its read-backs of this one found the re-proof
route (now the per-segment, disclosure-counted `rho_out`), an overstated
residual and a wallet-local counter a seed rebuild would lose. Limits:
refusal of service stays open: an operator that holds or refuses a settle,
or returns before a waiting release and refuses its re-proof, leaves the
acceptance unreleased in the record, and the count (C2b.5.2) does not reach
it where the notes' spends are admitted or fewer than `m` requests are
filed. A backer that names one owner for two demands of one quantity bears
the dishonour a taken release leaves. No reference wallet builds settlements
yet. A C3.8 reader keeps, for each output, the demand of the settlement that
inserted it.

**Spec change:** [money-from-first-principles 740adaa](https://github.com/mediumofexchange/money-from-first-principles/commit/740adaa)
(pool-recovery C3.4, C3.5, C3.8 and §8; pool-authority C2.10.8).

## 2026-10-01 — Verify a trail's proofs ahead of its replay on a pool of verifier instances

**Status:** accepted (v3 candidate reader; M5b.6 of the
[storage decision](2026-09.md#2026-09-29--keep-replay-state-in-each-partys-sqlite-storage-committed-at-keep-points)).

**Question:** M5b.6 asks for proof verification off the replay, so that a
reader's first sync fits 24 h at the design point
([budgets](../docs/PRODUCTION_REQUIREMENTS.md#target-scale-and-budgets)).
The whole process, verifier backends included, must stay within 1 GiB, and
the first failing check and the handling of a verifier's throw must not
change. The replay asked for each proof when a record's judgment reached its
proof check and waited for it. Each backend instance already verifies in a
WASM worker thread of its own, so the reader's thread sat idle for the
proof, and the worker sat idle for the replay.

**Decision:**
- *Declared parallelism.* A proof verifier may declare `parallel`: how many
  verifications it runs at once off the reader's thread. Where one is
  declared, `replayTrail` starts later records' proofs while it judges and
  applies the current record (`src/pool/v3/verify-ahead.ts`).
- *A verdict belongs to its bytes.* A started verdict goes only to a
  judgment that asks for exactly the kind, public inputs and proof bytes
  verified, and only at that record's proof check. A verifier's throw, at
  the call or later, surfaces at that record's proof check. A failure
  reading the trail surfaces when its record is due.
- *Bounds.* The window is two started proofs per declared verification, at
  most 128. A replay keeps no more proofs started and not yet asked for than
  its judgments have been handed as valid. So it starts nothing before its
  first proof verifies, and it drops at most what it used. The verifier one
  read binds holds at most the window of started verifications, across every
  replay of that read.
- *Undeclared verifiers are unchanged.* A verifier declaring nothing, such
  as a test stub or a counting wrapper, is asked in turn as before.
- *The pool.* `proofVerifier` takes `instances`, from 1 to 64, each a WASM
  worker of its own that is retired after its own throw. A call goes to the
  instance with the fewest calls waiting, and `parallel` equals `instances`.
  The journal, the wallet and the package reader carry the declared value
  when they bind the caller's verifier.
- *Barretenberg's Poseidon2* stays an untaken lever. The measured first sync
  fits the budget without it (see Evidence).

**Rationale:**
- *Only proofs parallelize.* Each record is judged against the state its
  predecessor left, so the replay itself is sequential. A proof's verdict
  depends on its own bytes alone. bb.js already runs each instance's WASM in
  a worker, so this adds no worker code or message format of its own.
- *A batch pass first* (every proof of a trail verified before the replay)
  would let a later record's PROOF refusal pre-empt an earlier record's
  CONTEXT refusal. Holding its verdicts until their records are reached is
  this lookahead with an unbounded window: memory in the trail's length, and
  every proof after a refusal verified for nothing.
- *Unconditional speculation* was the first form. An independent review
  found that an excluded checkpoint then dropped a full window of
  verifications while using none. The dropped calls piled up across replays
  on the verifier's queues, without backpressure: 12,800 pending calls at
  `parallel` 64 over 100 refused replays of one 200-record trail, holding
  1.6 GB of proof copies. The earned bound and the per-read cap remove this.
  The same reproducer now leaves no call pending. What a hostile operator can
  induce is at most twice the verification its published checkpoints already
  demand.
- *More threads inside one instance* (`threads`) were not taken. Instances
  were measured to scale, and a verification is short and mostly sequential.
- *Barretenberg's Poseidon2* computes the note tree's node hash in 0.12 ms,
  against 1.14 ms in JavaScript, with equal outputs over 2,000 chained
  hashes. It would cut the replay's largest cost about ninefold, for every
  party. It needs either a second host implementation of the in-circuit
  hash or a hard bb.js dependency in the note tree, with an initialized
  WASM instance before any hash. Neither is needed for the budget measured
  here; it stays in WORK.md's conditional list.

**Evidence:**
- *Measurements:* the [probe section](../docs/POOL_DEPLOYMENT_PROBES.md#verification-ahead-and-the-first-sync-m5b6).
  On this 2-core desktop, one verification takes 30.5 ms. The replay's own
  work is about 40 ms a record with a stub verifier. A replay with real
  verification in turn took 80.5 ms a record; ahead on one instance it took
  51.5 ms. A first sync of 10⁵ statements, verified ahead on two instances
  into a kept file, replayed at 52.7 ms a statement with a 543 MB peak. At
  10⁶, extrapolated, it takes about 15.5–16 h here, or about 20.5 h with
  the Ergo header check run after it.
- *Tests:*
  - `test/pool-v3-verify-ahead.test.ts` covers the ramp, the bound on dropped
    work, the per-read cap, the adopted block and undecodable records, and
    the deferred read failure.
  - `test/pool-v3-kept-state.test.ts` reads a history with REPEATED_STATEMENT
    (before PROOF), PROOF and SIGNATURE (after PROOF) exclusions, ahead and
    in turn, and requires equal outcomes and equal proof counts. It also
    surfaces a verifier's throw identically on 1 and 3 instances and at the
    call.
  - `scripts/pool/v3/check.mjs` verifies concurrent calls on a two-instance
    verifier through its own throws and its close.
  - `check:pool:v3-history`'s fresh reader verifies ahead on two instances,
    with exact proof counts.
- *Independent review:* one reviewer, with a read-back of the fixes. Taken:
  the earned bound and the per-read cap (material), the deferred read
  failure (minor), a function thenable counted toward the window, and the
  cap stated per read, not per verifier object. Left: `MAX_INSTANCES`
  (64) is a sanity cap, not a budget; the parties' commands choose
  instances against the measured memory per instance (slice 9).
  The window ramps up again in every replay, so a checkpoint of fewer
  records than the window never reaches full width.
- *Limits:* one desktop with 2 physical cores, below the reader's declared
  8. The real verification cycles through eight issue proofs, not the
  records' own. The design point is extrapolated from 10⁵ statements using
  the storage probe's growth to 10⁶.

**Spec change:** none needed. Verification order within a replay is
unchanged (pool-v3 §7.1, §14).
